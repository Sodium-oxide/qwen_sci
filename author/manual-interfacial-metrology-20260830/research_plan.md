# Beyond Film Thickness: A Design-Only Research Plan for Correlative Geometric--Molecular--Force Metrology of Nanoscale Film Stability

**Disciplines:** Chemical Engineering; Chemistry

## Abstract

Microscopic interfaces govern drainage, wetting, reaction, and heat or mass transfer, yet a film-thickness trace does not by itself identify the molecular or mechanical causes of an interfacial transition. This research plan integrates a manual literature Survey, an Idea-stage causal hypothesis, and a design-only ExperimentDesign artifact into a single proposal for non-biological liquid--solid nanoscale thin-film interfaces. Its central proposition is conditional: within an expert-approved, geometry-matched comparison boundary, a temporally coherent relation among film geometry, an interface-specific molecular-state representation, and a local confined-interaction representation may distinguish chemistry-mediated disjoining-pressure control of film drainage or stability from a thickness-only explanation.

The proposed contribution is an evidence architecture rather than an experimental result. It requires a calibrated geometric baseline, a molecular-state channel with explicit interpretation limits, a local force or disjoining-pressure proxy with an explicit perturbation audit, a declared transition outcome, and a common time/uncertainty representation. It compares a geometry-only account with single-added-channel and joint-channel accounts, while retaining capillarity, optical-model assumptions, surface history, roughness, probe effects, observation-induced change, and temporal mismatch as live alternatives. Four conditional outcome branches prevent a raw cross-channel correlation from being treated as causal evidence. No material system, chemical identity, instrument configuration, operating parameter, physical procedure, or observed result is supplied. Any physical implementation remains blocked pending qualified chemical-safety, facility, calibration, and model-system review.

**Keywords:** interfacial metrology; nanoscale liquid films; sum-frequency generation; confined forces; disjoining pressure; causal discrimination; research design.

## 1. Introduction

Gas--liquid, liquid--solid, and solid--solid interfaces are sites at which geometry, molecular organization, and transport couple on different spatial and temporal scales. Optical interference and ellipsometric methods provide valuable access to nanometre-scale film geometry, but thickness is only one state variable. It does not uniquely identify molecular orientation, adsorption, local confined interactions, or the route by which a film drains, arrests, persists, or ruptures. The Survey underlying this proposal therefore treats microscopic interfacial measurement as a problem of **mechanism discrimination**, not merely resolution improvement.

The relevant literature provides complementary component capabilities. Nonlinear optical spectroscopy can constrain interfacial molecular arrangement; quantitative interpretation of a vibrational representation requires explicit orientational and spectral assumptions [1,2]. Surface-force approaches can supply local confined-force and reactivity evidence, but introduce probe, roughness, and interaction-model limitations [3]. Imaging ellipsometry can provide a non-contact geometric anchor for nanometre-scale liquid films [4]. These capabilities motivate a correlative architecture. They do not establish that a combined molecular--force relation has already caused a stability transition in one specified system.

This manuscript is a research plan, not an empirical report. It does not select a liquid, a solid, a chemical formulation, an instrument, a parameter range, or an experimental procedure. The design is restricted to future expert-approved **non-biological** model interfaces. Its value is to make the causal claim, the competing explanations, the required evidence, and the conditions under which no conclusion is permitted explicit before any future implementation is considered.

## 2. Literature-grounded gap and research objective

### 2.1 Why thickness-only evidence is non-identifying

Thickness may change during an interfacial event for many reasons. Geometry, curvature, capillary forcing, refractive-index assumptions, surface history, contamination, roughness, and an observation perturbation can each produce a time-varying thickness record without establishing a chemistry-mediated local-interaction mechanism. A mechanism claim therefore cannot be justified by temporal co-occurrence between a spectral feature and a thickness transition alone.

The Survey identifies three complementary evidence roles. First, a geometric representation anchors the state of the film and permits a thickness-only baseline. Second, an interface-specific molecular representation constrains possible changes in local organization but must retain its spectral-inversion and orientational limits [1,2]. Third, a local confined-interaction representation constrains whether the interaction landscape changes consistently with the molecular representation [3]. The joint use of these roles creates a decision problem: does the combined account explain the declared film transition more adequately than geometry alone while known artifact alternatives remain insufficient?

### 2.2 Objective

The objective is to formulate a falsifiable, auditable research plan for a correlative geometric--molecular--force comparison at a non-biological liquid--solid nanoscale interface. The plan does not optimize an interface or predict a performance value. Instead, it asks whether a future, safety-approved evidence record could discriminate the following two explanations:

1. **Geometry-only account:** film geometry, curvature, and capillary forcing account for the declared drainage or stability transition.
2. **Chemistry-mediated account:** a change in interface-specific molecular state changes a local confined interaction or disjoining-pressure contribution, adding explanatory information beyond geometry alone.

## 3. Central proposition, scope, and falsifier

### 3.1 Conditional proposition

The central proposition is: *within a bounded, non-biological liquid--solid thin-film system, a temporally coherent relation among film geometry, an interface-specific vibrational state, and a confined-force proxy can distinguish chemistry-mediated disjoining-pressure control of drainage or stability from a thickness-only explanation.*

For planning purposes, represent the candidate interfacial state at a relative event time \(t\) as

\[
\mathbf{X}(t)=\bigl[G(t),\;M(t),\;F(t),\;T(t),\;A(t)\bigr],
\]

where \(G\) denotes calibrated geometry, \(M\) an interface-specific molecular-state representation, \(F\) a local confined-interaction representation, \(T\) a declared transition outcome, and \(A\) the audit record for calibration, surface history, perturbation, and temporal registration. This notation is an organizing device, not a claim that all quantities are already measurable, commensurate, or causally complete.

The geometry-only baseline and the joint account can be represented conceptually as

\[
\mathcal{M}_0:T\leftarrow G,\qquad \mathcal{M}_1:T\leftarrow (G,M,F,A).
\]

The plan does not predefine a numerical threshold for preferring \(\mathcal{M}_1\). Any future criterion must be fixed after qualified experts approve the model system, calibration traceability, uncertainty model, and estimand. The evidence cannot support a mechanism merely because \(M\), \(F\), and \(T\) change in proximity.

### 3.2 Scope and non-claims

The proposal applies only to a future expert-approved liquid--solid non-biological model interface and one declared geometry-matched comparison boundary. It does not claim a universal law for all liquids, solids, interfacial chemistries, gas--liquid systems, evaporative devices, or industrial conditions. It does not claim that a molecular-state channel, a force channel, or an optical channel independently proves causation.

The proposition is falsified or narrowed if a geometry-only account predicts the transition equally well; if molecular and force representations fail to add temporally coherent discriminating information; or if optical calibration, surface history, capillarity, roughness, probe perturbation, interaction-model error, observation-induced chemistry, or time-registration uncertainty fully accounts for the apparent relation.

## 4. Research design and evidence architecture

### 4.1 Required representations

| Evidence role | Design requirement | Why it is needed | Major limitation retained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Geometric state | A calibrated, time-indexed film thickness and/or morphology representation | Establishes the physical state and the geometry-only baseline | Optical model and refractive-index assumptions |
| Molecular state | An interface-specific vibrational or chemical-state representation | Constrains possible molecular reorganization or adsorption | Spectral, phase, and orientational interpretation limits |
| Local interaction | A confined-force or disjoining-pressure representation | Constrains the candidate interaction landscape | Probe perturbation, roughness, and interaction-model error |
| Transition outcome | A human-approved definition of drainage, arrest, persistence, or rupture | States what competing accounts must explain | Endpoint remains system-dependent |
| Audit record | Calibration, surface history, perturbation, and time-registration record | Blocks artifact-driven causal claims | Completeness must be assessed before interpretation |

The evidence roles are complementary rather than interchangeable. A geometric record without molecular or force context remains under-identifying. A spectral or force change without a geometry-matched comparison can remain a response to a different state. A complete record may still be insufficient if the audit cannot bound a named alternative.

### 4.2 Logical comparison framework

The design defines four **explanatory accounts**, not physical sample recipes:

| Account | Included representations | Intended comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C0: geometry-only | \(G,T,A\) | Baseline explanation of the transition |
| C1: geometry + molecular | \(G,M,T,A\) | Tests whether molecular state adds information beyond C0 |
| C2: geometry + local interaction | \(G,F,T,A\) | Tests whether local interaction adds information beyond C0 |
| C3: joint account | \(G,M,F,T,A\) | Tests whether a coherent joint account improves upon C0--C2 |

Any future comparison must preserve a declared comparability boundary rather than presume that records acquired by different channels are automatically equivalent. The boundary must state how geometry, time, surface history, and measurement perturbation are represented. The plan deliberately avoids specifying laboratory actions because these decisions depend on the approved model system and facility.

### 4.3 Audit-first decision rule

The proposal uses an audit-first rule. A causal interpretation becomes eligible only when all of the following are documented: (i) a geometry-only explanatory baseline; (ii) stated uncertainty for geometric, molecular, and interaction representations; (iii) a justified cross-channel time basis; (iv) a surface-history and roughness record; and (v) a probe/observation-perturbation assessment. Failure of any condition selects the non-interpretable branch rather than a provisional mechanism conclusion.

## 5. Analysis, robustness, and reproducibility plan

### 5.1 Planned explanatory comparison

After expert approval of an estimand, the planned analysis would compare the explanatory adequacy of \(\mathcal{M}_0\), \(\mathcal{M}_1\), and the single-added-channel accounts under the same registered comparability boundary. The report would need to retain uncertainty and model assumptions rather than reduce evidence to a single raw correlation. No statistic, effect magnitude, acceptance threshold, allocation sequence, repeat count, or operating condition is fixed in this manuscript.

The most important robustness checks are conceptual ablations. Removing the molecular-state representation asks whether the claim collapses to geometry. Removing the local-interaction representation asks whether the molecular representation remains underdetermined. Varying approved calibration and registration assumptions asks whether the interpretation is stable. Treating surface history, roughness, probe effects, and observation-induced changes as sensitivity cases prevents them from disappearing after the preferred explanation is chosen.

### 5.2 Provenance and reproducibility

Reproducibility is defined here as traceability of the future comparison logic, not replication of an unperformed physical measurement. A downstream record must preserve raw and derived channel representations, calibration metadata, time-registration decisions, surface-history metadata, uncertainty assumptions, comparison-model versions, exclusions, and outcome-branch selection. It must also preserve the geometry-only baseline so that later work cannot silently reframe a correlation as a mechanism.

The literature provenance is intentionally qualified. The upstream Survey was manually produced and has no automatic Survey-to-Idea manifest binding. Its selected cross-validated anchors support component measurement capabilities and limitations; they do not establish the novel cross-modality causal relation. The plan therefore carries the status `survey_unbound_requires_human_confirmation` throughout the pipeline.

## 6. Conditional outcome branches

| Branch | Future trigger | Permitted interpretation | Required next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supports mechanism | A prespecified comparison finds added, coherent joint explanatory information beyond geometry, and named artifact alternatives are insufficient | Bounded evidence would be consistent with chemistry-mediated local-interaction control in the documented model boundary | Independent replication and expert review before any broader claim |
| Partial or heterogeneous | The relation is informative only for a subset of declared states or audits | The mechanism may be conditional or incompletely identifiable | Refine state taxonomy and inspect heterogeneity sources |
| Null or contradictory | Geometry explains equally well, or added channels conflict | The proposed mechanism is not supported in the documented boundary | Report the null/contradiction and revise the Idea separately if needed |
| Uninformative or invalid | A critical calibration, comparability, perturbation, or timing audit is absent or non-discriminating | No causal conclusion is permitted | Stop interpretation and redesign the evidence architecture |

These branches prohibit a null or invalid result from being reframed as latent support. They also prevent a conditional result from becoming a universal chemical or engineering claim.

## 7. Responsible-research boundary

The ExperimentDesign artifact is `DESIGN_ONLY` and its risk gate is `BLOCKED_BY_RISK_REVIEW`. No people, animals, plants, clinical populations, environmental samples, or restricted biological systems are included. No material identity, chemical formulation, apparatus configuration, quantity, operating parameter, or physical procedure is provided.

If and only if a future physical route is considered, qualified reviewers must first confirm chemical safety, facility compatibility, model-system appropriateness, calibration traceability, and governance requirements. Those decisions are `needs_human_input`. This manuscript cannot be used as an authorization for physical experimentation.

## 8. Expected contribution, limitations, and conclusion

The proposed contribution is a disciplined shift from thickness measurement to evidence-architecture design. It defines a finite scientific object, a causal alternative set, a geometry-only baseline, complementary molecular and local-interaction representations, and explicit no-information conditions. This structure is useful even if a future review decides that no physical implementation is appropriate: it identifies which missing evidence prevents a mechanism claim.

The central limitation is that the proposal remains unexecuted and model-system agnostic. Cross-channel spatial and temporal comparability, molecular-state specificity, force-proxy specificity, and transferability beyond one model interface are unresolved. The manual Survey provenance also requires human confirmation. These limitations are not defects to be hidden; they are the decision boundaries that preserve the paper's proposal status.

In conclusion, this research plan does not assert that chemistry-mediated disjoining pressure has been established. It specifies the evidence conditions under which a future non-biological study could discriminate that account from thickness-only explanations, or could correctly conclude that the available evidence is null, contradictory, or non-interpretable.
