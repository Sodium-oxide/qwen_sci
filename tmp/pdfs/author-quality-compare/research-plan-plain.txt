Abstract—This proposal introduces an AANEC-relaxed
trapped surface theorem designed to establish a compactness
bound for non-black-hole stellar remnants within a restricted
model class of spherically symmetric, regular spacetimes with
complete null generators. The planned contribution replaces

I. Introduction
A. Compact Remnants and the Stellar Mass Gap
The observed stellar mass gap is a central feature in
the population of compact remnants, yet its physical
origin remains debated. Gravitational-wave observations
and stellar-evolution modeling indicate that the location of
the lower edge of the black hole mass gap is dictated by the
near-final core mass, which in turn sets the resulting black
hole mass. This suggests that the gap is astrophysically
inferred and governed by progenitor physics rather than
being a direct consequence of a pure general-relativistic
theorem. Consequently, the gap serves as a consistency
constraint for models of massive-star collapse, requiring
a clear separation between formal geometric bounds and
the complex stellar-physics mechanisms that populate the
observed remnant distribution. [1], [2]
B. Alternative Explanations and Confounders
While geometric bounds may restrict the final state of
collapse, the observed distribution of remnant masses is
heavily influenced by alternative stellar-physics mechanisms. Numerical simulations of binary mergers demonstrate that the use of a non-isentropic equation of state
leads to delayed collapse to a black hole, particularly in
high-mass binaries, due to pressure support from temperature increases via shocks. Furthermore, stellar-evolution
codes incorporating diffusion for angular momentum and
chemical abundances enable calculations of rotating-star
models, highlighting how rotation and mixing can alter

collapse outcomes. These findings indicate that explosion
energetics, fallback, equation-of-state uncertainties, and
binary interactions act as confounders that can populate
or avoid the stellar mass gap independently of a trappedsurface theorem. [3], [4]
C. Proposal Scope and Claim Discipline
This proposal introduces a formal theorem for classical
general relativity under restricted conditions, specifically
spherical nonrotating collapse with isotropic pressure.
The central contribution is to replace pointwise energy
conditions with the Averaged Null Energy Condition
(AANEC) combined with an explicit averaged nullconvergence assumption on null congruence generators.
This formal mechanism is designed to derive a trappedsurface threshold via Raychaudhuri-type focusing and
Misner-Sharp compactness. Crucially, the proposal maintains strict claim discipline: the observed 2-5 solar mass
gap is not assumed to be a direct consequence of this
theorem. Instead, it is treated as an astrophysical consistency constraint, subject to the alternative mechanisms
identified in the literature.
D. Design Assumptions and Boundary Conditions
The validity of the proposed formal bound depends
on several design assumptions. The model class assumes
globally hyperbolic spacetime with regular matter fields
and complete null generators. It further assumes that nonblack-hole remnants are modeled as stable equilibrium objects obeying a bounded compactness limit. The theorem is
expected to fail or require restriction if AANEC is violated,
if the added averaged null-convergence condition fails, or
if nonspherical, rotating, or magnetic dynamics dominate
the collapse. These boundary conditions define the scope
of the formal contribution, separating it from the broader
astrophysical inference required to connect the bound to
the observed mass gap.
II. Background, Survey, and Research Gap
A. Theoretical Foundations and Observational Context
Black holes serve as both exact solutions to Einstein’s field equations and empirically verified compact
objects, creating a unique intersection between theoretical physics and observational astrophysics. Establishing
their existence requires reconciling the stringent conditions predicted by singularity theorems with multimessenger observational benchmarks. The survey methodology delineates operational boundaries for black hole

identification, distinguishing between theoretical models,
astrophysical candidates, and confirmed objects through
dynamical mass constraints and horizon-scale diagnostics.
This framework systematically examines astrophysical
pathways leading to black hole formation, ranging from
core collapse to hierarchical assembly, while evaluating
observational proxies such as electromagnetic signatures,
stellar kinematics, and gravitational-wave ringdowns.
B. Evolution of Energy Conditions and AANEC
The theoretical edifice supporting black hole formation
is anchored in Penrose-type singularity theorems, which
demonstrate geodesic incompleteness contingent upon
specific energy conditions and the formation of trapped
surfaces. Traditionally, these theorems rely on the Null
Energy Condition (NEC) to guarantee the focusing of null
geodesics via the Raychaudhuri equation. However, the
introduction of quantum field theory reveals a profound
tension with these classical foundations, as pointwise
energy conditions are fundamentally incompatible with
quantum mechanics. Quantum fields routinely exhibit
regions of negative energy density, violating the Weak,
Strong, and Null Energy Conditions. To navigate this
conflict, the conceptual framework has shifted toward
constraints on averaged quantities. The Achronal Averaged Null Energy Condition (AANEC) has emerged as a
candidate for a universal property of gravitating matter,
offering a robust pathway to preserve singularity theorems
in the face of quantum violations by averaging along
achronal null geodesics.
C. Alternative Compact Objects and Counterexamples
Contemporary analyses reveal that the inevitability
of singularities is strictly contingent upon specific assumptions regarding energy conditions, causality, and
boundary configurations. A diverse class of horizonless
alternatives, known as Exotic Compact Objects (ECOs),
has been developed to address the singularity problem.
These models resolve infinities while closely mimicking
the observable signatures of astrophysical black holes. The
survey highlights that realistic matter fields, such as scalar
fields involved in inflationary scenarios, frequently violate
the Strong Energy Condition (SEC) while satisfying the
Dominant Energy Condition (DEC), thereby permitting
non-singular bouncing cosmologies and collapse scenarios.
Furthermore, the absence of trapped surfaces entirely
circumvents singularity theorems, allowing for singularityfree histories. This theoretical flexibility necessitates rigorous interrogation of alternative compact objects through
model-independent parametrizations, such as the closeness
parameter, which quantifies the proximity of an object’s
surface to the Schwarzschild radius.
D. Stellar-Mass Black Hole Formation and the Mass Gap
The transition from stellar core collapse to either a
neutron star or a black hole is governed by progenitor

structure, explosion dynamics, and neutrino physics. Recent theoretical frameworks have moved away from relying
solely on zero-age main sequence mass, instead emphasizing post-bounce compactness metrics as robust discriminators for collapse outcomes. Stars with high compactness
tend to undergo rapid accretion that overwhelms neutrino
heating, leading to failing supernovae and direct black
hole formation. Conversely, lower compactness progenitors
allow suﬀicient time for instabilities to reverse the shock.
The observed paucity of compact objects in the 2-5 solar
mass range provides a crucial observational constraint on
these scenarios. The existence of this mass gap strongly
favors rapid explosion dynamics for single stars, where the
dichotomy between successful explosions and total failures
leaves no room for intermediate remnants. However, the
survey notes that theoretical predictions remain sensitive
to uncertainties in explosion and unbinding energies,
leaving key physical parameters unconstrained.
E. Observational Proxies and Strong-Field Tests
Testing General Relativity in the strong-field regime
constitutes a fundamental probe of gravity’s validity beyond the weak-field limit. The survey synthesizes insights
from dynamical mass measurements in X-ray binaries,
horizon-scale interferometry, and gravitational-wave astronomy. Dynamical mass measurements constitute the
most direct method for confirming stellar-mass black
holes, establishing rigorous lower bounds on compact
object masses. These measurements have revealed a narrow mass distribution clustered tightly around 7.8 solar
masses, challenging conventional supernova fallback scenarios. Additionally, gravitational-wave observations offer
clean tests of strong-field gravity by probing coherent
source motions. The non-detection of gravitational-wave
echoes and the absence of observed highly-spinning compact objects impose severe restrictions on the viability
of many horizonless models. Despite this, current data
does not definitively rule out all exotic alternatives due
to significant model dependence in deriving exclusion
bounds.

central hypothesis and claim scope. The proposal replaces

Appendix B
Variables, Symbols, and Operational Definitions
A. Geometric and Matter-Field Symbols
The formal argument is stated within a restricted
model class defined by a specific set of geometric and

matter-field symbols. The spacetime metric is denoted
by g, and the stress-energy tensor by T. Null geodesic
generators are represented by their tangent vector k,
while the expansion scalar of a null geodesic congruence
is denoted by theta. These symbols support the core
assumptions: spherical symmetry and isotropic pressure
(A1), the Averaged Null Energy Condition along complete
null generators (A2), global hyperbolicity and regularity
(A3), null generator completeness (A4), and an explicit
averaged null-convergence or focusing condition (A5).
The mass of the collapsing matter or remnant object is
symbolized by M, and the areal radius or radial coordinate
by r. These definitions provide the language for the
proposed trapped-surface derivation, but their operational
role depends on resolving the specific model-dependent
thresholds discussed below.
B. The Misner-Sharp Compactness Parameter
A central element of the proposed theorem is the
Misner-Sharp compactness parameter, symbolized by C.
This quantity is intended to serve as the threshold variable
that, when exceeded, forces trapped-surface formation under the stated focusing and energy-condition assumptions.
However, the specific formal definition of C, including
the formula for effective mass within radius r in the
chosen metric, is not supplied. Without this formula,
the compactness threshold remains a placeholder rather
than a computable quantity, and the derivation of the
exclusion bound for stable non-black-hole remnants cannot
proceed. This gap is tracked as an unresolved premise
requiring human input before the formal argument can be
completed.
C. Operational Variables Requiring Specification
Several variables that mediate between the formal theorem and its astrophysical interpretation lack operational
definitions. The measurement method for stellar remnant
mass, which could involve radial velocity curves or pulsar
timing, is unspecified, as is the specific metric for explosion
energy, whether kinetic or total, and its measurement
in observed samples. Additionally, the equation-of-state
models or nuclear physics parameters to be tested are
not listed, and the measurement or initial condition
specifications for rotation and magnetic fields in collapse
models are undefined. These omissions mean that the
proposed variables V6 through V12 cannot yet be operationalized for empirical comparison, and their treatment
as confounders or consistency constraints remains a design
assumption pending human specification.
Appendix C
Evidence Coverage, Unknown Items, and Review
Checklist
A. Bounded Evidence Coverage
The proposal’s reliance on averaged energy conditions
is bounded by the finding that pointwise energy conditions are systematically violated by quantum fields,

which necessitates weaker statements such as quantum
energy inequalities or averaged energy conditions for
broader validity. This supports the design choice to replace
pointwise NEC or SEC assumptions with AANEC, but it
does not prove that AANEC alone implies the additional
explicit averaged null-convergence condition required by
the theorem. Astrophysical context is supplied by the
observation that the lower edge of the black hole mass gap
is dictated by near-final core mass and is robust against
variations in metallicity, internal mixing, and stellar wind
mass loss. This anchors the mass-gap discussion in stellarphysics mechanisms rather than treating the gap as a
direct prediction of the formal theorem. [2], [5]
B. Formal Unknowns and Counterexample Limits
Two formal gaps prevent release of any theorem claim.
The Misner-Sharp compactness parameter C lacks a
supplied mathematical definition, so compactness thresholds and mass scales cannot be computed. The explicit
averaged null-convergence condition A5 likewise lacks a
formal mathematical form, so candidate witnesses cannot
be tested against it. Counterexample analysis therefore
remains a boundary case rather than a completed result:
a proposed witness is a static, spherically symmetric,
regular metric with isotropic pressure and high compactness satisfying AANEC, but its status under A5 and
under the stability predicate is unknown. Because the
counterexample must satisfy all assumptions, its validity
cannot be decided until A5 and C are formally specified.
C. Release Criteria for Future Claims
•

Before any formal claim is released, human review
must confirm the following: first, supply the mathematical definition of Misner-Sharp compactness C
and its relation to the model-dependent threshold;
second, supply the mathematical form of the explicit
averaged null-convergence condition A5 and verify
whether it is independent of AANEC; third, audit
the proposed counterexample witnesses to determine
whether they satisfy A5 and the stability predicate or
instead violate an assumption; fourth, confirm that
all empirical mass-gap statements remain separated
from the formal theorem and are treated only as
astrophysical consistency constraints.
