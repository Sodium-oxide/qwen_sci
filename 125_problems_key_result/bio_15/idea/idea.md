# Idea Portfolio and Direction Selection

## Evidence-bound starting point

The Survey establishes that a global population peak is a conditional projection output. Official and independent studies use component models and uncertainty distributions, and they do not support a single inevitable answer to the question of indefinite growth. The selected problem is consequently a forecasting and decision problem: identify the conditions under which posterior global annual growth remains positive at the horizon, then test whether the information used to make that statement is calibrated.

Only the accepted Survey gaps seed this portfolio. The rejected assertion that population methods ignore uncertainty is not reused. The uncertain climate item is retained as a pre-specified ablation, not as an assumed causal relation.

## Candidate comparison

The first route is a projection reconciliation dashboard. It would compare published total-population curves and peak years. This is useful communication, but it cannot tell whether a disagreement arises from fertility, survival, migration, starting age structure, calibration, or a hidden assumption. It is therefore not sufficient for the primary gap.

The second route is the Component-Resolved Population Peak Persistence Atlas. It retains cohort-component accounting but makes each component a separate uncertainty-bearing process. Its final output is a posterior probability of positive global growth through 2100, a distribution of first peak years, and a sensitivity decomposition. The study would compare a demographic baseline against a model that adds pre-specified climate-disruption features. This route directly covers both accepted gaps and can fail transparently.

The third route would regress long-run world population on a global climate index. It appears simple but is scientifically invalid under the Survey boundary. Climate-related effects operate through heterogeneous local pathways, adaptation, development, conflict, and measurement. A single global coefficient would confound mechanisms and convert an unresolved issue into a result by construction.

## Selected primary idea

The selected direction is the Component-Resolved Population Peak Persistence Atlas. For country c, age a, sex s, and year t, the model will propagate uncertainty in age-specific fertility, survival, and net migration through a coherent population accounting system. Global growth is derived only after country trajectories are aggregated. Climate-disruption variables are optional, lagged covariates in component submodels; a no-climate model remains the primary baseline.

The central hypothesis is conditional and falsifiable: a joint component ensemble can distinguish posterior scenarios that sustain positive global growth after 2100 from scenarios that peak before 2100, and climate-disruption covariates will be retained only if they improve held-out predictive scoring and calibration. The hypothesis does not predict that either branch will be observed.

## Falsification and feasibility

The direction fails if it cannot pass rolling-origin calibration, if a simpler baseline predicts held-out demographic outcomes at least as well, if component attributions are unstable across cutoffs or aggregation levels, or if climate variables add no reliable predictive value. These are productive failures: they would show that post-2100 persistence cannot be supported by this specification.

The proposal is feasible as a computational design because WPP provides a country-level demographic accounting base. It still requires human review before any execution. Reviewers must confirm data-license terms, age and sex harmonization, treatment of disputed territories, scenario definitions, prior sensitivity, and the boundary between prediction and causal interpretation.
