# Survey Agent: From Human-Unique Genes to Testable Cortical Mechanisms

## Scientific reframing

The productive question is not whether one or a few genes “make us human.” Nearly all human protein-coding genes have recognizable primate orthologues, while coding changes, segmental duplications, deletions, noncoding substitutions, and changes in three-dimensional genome organization coexist. This survey reframes the topic as: **which human-lineage genetic changes causally alter a defined developmental transition in the cerebral cortex, through which molecular path, and under what evidence conditions can that effect be generalized beyond one cell line or organoid protocol?**

The proposed target transition is the balance among apical radial glia, basal radial glia, intermediate progenitors, and newborn excitatory neurons during early corticogenesis. This target is narrow enough to measure, evolutionary enough to be meaningful, and avoids the invalid leap from a cell phenotype to “human intelligence.”

## Evidence map

1. **Human-specific coding and copy-number changes can be functional candidates.** Human-specific duplicates such as `ARHGAP11B`, `NOTCH2NL`, and `CROCCP2` are plausible because they enter conserved cortical-progenitor pathways, but their existence does not prove that each is a universal driver of human cortical evolution.
2. **Regulatory divergence is often more numerous than coding divergence.** Human accelerated regions (HARs), human-specific deletions, and altered enhancer--promoter contacts can change developmental gene regulation. HAR sequence effects may be compensatory and cell-state dependent; a sequence difference therefore requires assay-specific evidence.
3. **Comparative organoids reveal developmental timing and state differences, but do not erase model limitations.** Great-ape cerebral organoids can align developmental trajectories, resolve cell states, and support paired perturbations. Their regional composition, maturation, stress state, and batch composition must be checked before causal interpretation.
4. **Direct perturbation supplies stronger evidence than cross-species association.** In hominid organoids, `ARHGAP11B` gain in chimpanzee and loss/interference in human systems altered basal-progenitor abundance. This is a concrete causal result for a defined phenotype, not a complete account of human uniqueness.
5. **A causal claim must traverse a chain.** A candidate sequence should be linked to a molecular alteration, a reproducible cell-state transition, and a reciprocal perturbation result. Observing differential expression alone is insufficient.

## Evidence boundaries

| Evidence class | Permitted statement | Not permitted |
|---|---|---|
| Comparative genome sequence | A locus is a human-lineage candidate | The locus causes a human trait |
| Human/chimp organoid expression or accessibility | A state-specific association is present in the model | The association is a cell-autonomous genetic effect |
| Reporter, chromatin-contact, or editing evidence | The tested allele or regulatory element changes the measured molecular readout | A measured molecular effect explains all cortical evolution |
| Reciprocal humanization/reversion in qualified organoids | The altered module has conditional causal support for the predeclared cell-state endpoint | The module makes an organism uniquely human or determines cognition |

## Accepted research gaps

- **G1 -- Causal-chain gap:** candidate discovery, molecular mechanism, and cortical cell-state consequence are too often reported in separate studies.
- **G2 -- Variant-class gap:** duplicated genes and noncoding regulatory changes are frequently tested independently, even though they may converge on the same progenitor transition.
- **G3 -- Reciprocity gap:** one-direction gain-of-function experiments cannot separate a human-lineage allele effect from model-specific dosage effects.
- **G4 -- Model-fidelity gap:** cross-species organoid contrasts can be driven by line, batch, region composition, or maturity rather than the selected genetic difference.
- **G5 -- Claim-calibration gap:** reports can move too quickly from neural progenitor phenotypes to broad language, cognition, or species-identity claims.

## Survey conclusion and handoff

The most defensible next study is a **reciprocal, multiplex, cell-state-resolved comparison** of a small, evidence-qualified set of human-lineage duplicated and regulatory candidates. It should ask whether these candidates converge on the same progenitor-persistence to neurogenic-transition axis, while forcing negative controls, line-aware replication, organoid-fidelity gates, and abstention when the causal chain is incomplete.
