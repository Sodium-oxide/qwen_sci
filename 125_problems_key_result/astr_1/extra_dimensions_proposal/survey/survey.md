# Survey Agent Output: How Many Dimensions Are There in Space?

## Research framing

Humans observe three large spatial dimensions and one time dimension in ordinary laboratory, astronomical, and relativistic practice. That observation does not, by itself, answer how many dimensions are present in a fundamental physical theory. The phrase **extra dimensions** refers to several non-equivalent ideas: compact spatial dimensions in Kaluza--Klein theories; large or warped extra dimensions in effective gravitational models; dimensions required by perturbative string constructions; and phenomenological modifications to gravitational propagation. The survey therefore asks a narrower, falsifiable question:

> Which parameterized extra-dimension models make distinguishable predictions across short-range gravity, colliders, gravitational waves, and cosmology; and what combination of null results or anomaly patterns would exclude, retain, or render a model observationally unidentifiable?

This reframing prevents two opposite errors. First, the mathematical statement that a string construction is naturally formulated in a higher-dimensional spacetime is not evidence that those dimensions occur in nature. Second, a null result in one detector does not prove that no extra-dimensional model can exist. It constrains only a specified model, parameter region, and observable mapping.

## Evidence boundary

### Established observational baseline

At accessible scales, local physics is accurately modeled by one temporal and three extended spatial coordinates. General relativity and the Standard Model have been tested in this effective four-dimensional description across a vast range of scales. The survey treats this as the baseline to be challenged by a model-dependent signal, not as a proof that all additional dimensions are absent.

### Theory is plural, not a single prediction

Higher-dimensional formulations arise for different reasons. Kaluza--Klein constructions show how a compact dimension can produce lower-dimensional fields. Perturbative superstring theories are consistently formulated in ten spacetime dimensions, and M-theory is associated with an eleven-dimensional limit [S1], [S2]. The Arkani-Hamed--Dimopoulos--Dvali (ADD) proposal moves the hierarchy problem into large compact dimensions accessible primarily through gravity [S3]. Randall--Sundrum (RS) models use a warped fifth dimension and predict a different localization and mass-spectrum structure [S4], [S5].

None of these statements supplies a common numerical prediction such as ``there are exactly ten observable spatial dimensions.'' Compactification geometry, size, warping, brane content, coupling, stabilization, and ultraviolet completion determine which low-energy effects survive. A proposal must state which model class it tests before describing a measurement as a dimensional test.

### Observable channels are complementary

1. **Short-range gravity.** A compact or otherwise modified gravitational sector can generate departures from the inverse-square law at distances related to a compactification or crossover scale. Torsion-balance experiments therefore bound a common phenomenological Yukawa correction, but the translation from that correction to a number and size of dimensions is model-specific [S6].
2. **Collider data.** ADD-like scenarios can lead to missing-momentum signatures associated with inaccessible Kaluza--Klein graviton modes, while warped models can produce resonant or nonresonant signatures. Collider searches translate null results into limits on model scales only after choosing the effective theory, number of dimensions, and signal assumptions [S7], [S8].
3. **Astrophysical and cosmological observations.** Extra dimensions can alter early-universe, compact-object, and radiation histories. Such tests may be very sensitive but often depend strongly on thermal history, matter content, and model completion [S9].
4. **Gravitational waves.** Propagation from compact binaries can test altered damping, dispersion, or leakage in a phenomenological model. Multi-messenger and catalog-based analyses can constrain some propagation modifications, but a nonstandard damping measurement is not uniquely diagnostic of extra dimensions [S10], [S11].

## Source reliability and interpretation rules

The source registry differentiates mathematical/theoretical evidence, direct experimental likelihoods, and model translations. A formal consistency result supports only a statement about a theory's construction. A laboratory, collider, or gravitational-wave likelihood supports a bounded statement about its analysis model and parameter range. It does not settle all ultraviolet completions or all compactification geometries.

## Scientific synthesis

### A. ``How many dimensions?'' has an operational answer only after scales are named

At large scales, three spatial dimensions are observed. At shorter distances, the question becomes whether any experiment detects deviations from the four-dimensional effective description. A compact dimension of radius far below current resolution may leave no directly accessible geometrical image. Conversely, a large or warped dimension might alter a force law, particle spectrum, or propagation amplitude without being seen as a macroscopic direction. Thus, the number of dimensions is not a measurement independent of scale and model.

### B. Short-range gravity is direct but not universal

An often-used parameterization is

\[
V(r)=-\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r}\left[1+\alpha\exp\left(-\frac{r}{\lambda}\right)\right],
\]

where \(\alpha\) and \(\lambda\) describe the strength and range of a non-Newtonian component. This is valuable because it gives laboratory data an interpretable likelihood. It is not a universal extra-dimension equation: the mapping from \((\alpha,\lambda)\) to a compactification depends on geometry, source coupling, and other fields. A null torsion-balance result should therefore be recorded as a region of excluded Yukawa-like behavior and, separately, as a conditional model translation.

### C. Collider, gravitational-wave, and cosmological tests should not be conflated

Collider tests probe high-energy production and missing momentum or resonance spectra; gravitational-wave tests probe long-baseline propagation; cosmological tests probe history-dependent bulk or relic effects. These channels have different systematic errors and different degeneracies. Agreement among independent channels can be powerful, whereas a signal in one channel needs alternative explanations catalogued before it is described as extra-dimensional.

### D. Null results are information, but not a global no-go theorem

Current null searches disfavor parts of parameter space for selected ADD, RS, and phenomenological propagation models. They do not select one unique number of dimensions or rule out all hidden dimensions. The research gap is not simply sensitivity. It is the absence of an auditable, cross-scale protocol that states: which models are testable; which parameters each channel constrains; where sensitivity overlaps; which alternative explanations mimic a signal; and what result would actually exclude a model class under declared assumptions.

## Survey conclusion and handoff

The answer supported by the current evidence is precise: **three extended spatial dimensions are observed; additional compact or warped dimensions remain theoretical possibilities whose status is model- and parameter-dependent.** The evidence neither establishes an experimentally confirmed number of hidden dimensions nor supports a universal claim that they have been disproven.

The recommended Idea direction is **DimensionBridge**, a design-only cross-scale falsifiability atlas. It will separate models from observables, translate each experimental channel through declared assumptions, and require a candidate excess to survive alternative-explanation and cross-channel consistency tests. Its output will be a model status--`EXCLUDED_WITHIN_ASSUMPTIONS`, `CURRENTLY_COMPATIBLE`, `SENSITIVITY_LIMITED`, or `OBSERVATIONALLY_UNIDENTIFIABLE`--not a rhetorical yes/no answer.
