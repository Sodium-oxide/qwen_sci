# Idea Agent Result

## Primary direction

### Phylo-Developmental Speech Circuit Evolution Atlas (PDSCEA)

PDSCEA proposes that human speech did not originate from a newly appearing, isolated speech center. It emerged when older primate auditory and vocal circuits were progressively coupled to a human-specialized network for auditory-to-articulatory mapping, laryngeal and oro-facial control, predictive sensory feedback, and prolonged vocal learning. The common adult leftward language bias is treated as a developmental network property: useful and measurable, but neither universal nor equivalent to handedness.

The direction is intentionally stronger than a catalog of candidate brain regions. It makes a mechanistic claim that can fail: a model integrating conserved signal representations, frontotemporal auditory-motor connectivity, developmental age, and speech-specific temporal coupling should explain speech-learning and speech-control phenotypes better than models centered on one region, one gene, or one hemisphere.

## Search routes considered

### Route A: Single-region localization

This route would map speech to Broca's area, Wernicke's area, or a newly named voice area. It is easy to communicate but scientifically weak because it ignores task dependence, bilateral processing, distributed connectivity, and atypical lateralization. It is retained only as a negative-control model.

### Route B: FOXP2-centered evolution

FOXP2 and related developmental pathways are important candidate variables, particularly for speech-related motor learning. They cannot carry the entire explanation because speech phenotypes arise from interacting genes, circuits, anatomy, and learning. This route becomes a biological covariate and negative-control model, not the primary direction.

### Route C: Primate continuity

This route asks what human speech shares with primate communication-signal processing. It is essential for identifying conserved substrates, but vocalization and speech are not equivalent. It becomes the comparative component of PDSCEA.

### Route D: Developmental lateralization

This route models how language networks become more left-specialized in many people while remaining atypical or bilateral in others. It captures an important part of the puzzle but cannot alone explain the origin of vocal learning or the sensorimotor demands of speech. It becomes the developmental component of PDSCEA.

### Route E: Dynamic auditory-motor coupling

This route uses EEG/MEG, speech-envelope tracking, phase-locking, and perturbation of auditory feedback to test the timing of speech perception and production. It adds a mechanism rather than a location and becomes the temporal component of PDSCEA.

## MCTS-style synthesis

The search combines routes C, D, and E under the network constraint from Route A and the biological covariates from Route B. The combination is selected because it covers the five gaps in the Survey ledger without assuming that any single measurement is the historical origin of speech.

The highest-value test is a cross-species and developmental atlas with matched representational and connectivity features. Human speech tasks should be paired with non-speech auditory controls, nonhuman-primate conspecific vocalization tasks, and generic oro-facial motor controls. Cross-species comparisons should target representational geometry and homologous network relations rather than raw activation magnitudes.

## Portfolio

**Primary direction:** PDSCEA, combining ancient auditory-vocal substrate, human auditory-motor reorganization, developmental lateralization, and temporal coupling.

**Competitive direction:** A connectivity-first developmental model that predicts language lateralization from macroscale gradients and frontotemporal tract organization while treating cross-species vocal representations as priors.

**High-risk direction:** A multiscale causal model linking vocal learning, phase coupling, and gene-regulatory variation across species. It is potentially explanatory but difficult to identify with noninvasive data and should not be presented as a near-term causal proof.

**Rejected primary directions:** single-region localization, FOXP2-only explanation, left-hemisphere-only model, and direct use of a historical date such as 200,000 or 27 million years as an established speech origin.

## Final handoff

The downstream design should preregister: homologous regions and tract correspondences, task contrasts, lateralization index, dynamic coupling metrics, cross-species representational alignment, age trajectory, family structure, handedness covariates, and a model-comparison rule. It must report no observed outcomes because this artifact is a proposal, not an executed experiment.
