# Idea - PAN-CURE

**PAN-CURE** (Pan-cancer Antigen and Networked Cure Readiness Evaluation) is a design-only benchmark for evaluating whether a candidate immune-recognition strategy can support a bounded cross-cancer development claim. It does not seek a numerical ``universal cure score.'' Instead, it requires a declared indication set, target/recognition mechanism, normal-tissue boundary, delivery context, resistance family, and clinical endpoint hierarchy.

The central question is: for a stated immune modality and cancer set, does the intervention create reproducible, selective, durable anti-tumor activity across heterogeneous models and immune contexts without unacceptable normal-tissue or systemic toxicity? H0 attributes apparent breadth to model selection, target overexpression, assay artifacts, or selective reporting. H1 supports a bounded tumor-coverage and mechanism claim. H2 indicates activity in a subset only. H3 identifies escape, toxicity, or microenvironmental failure that blocks a broad claim.

PAN-CURE separates discovery, preclinical mechanism, translational qualification, and clinical benefit. It explicitly prohibits treating an in-vitro tumor-killing experiment, a response in one cancer, or a biomarker change as proof that all cancers can be cured.
