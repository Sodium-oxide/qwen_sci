# Idea Agent — Research Direction Portfolio

## Input contract

The Idea Agent accepted the Survey handoff only under its stated boundaries: giant size is treated as a repeated macroevolutionary outcome; pneumaticity and inferred feeding function remain proxies; and all directions must include a prediction that can fail outside the fitted fossil set.

## Candidate directions

### D1 — Vegetation-First Opportunity Map

Map paleofloral productivity and accessible browse height against sauropod mass through time. This direction directly addresses ecological opportunity, but it is **not selected** because vegetation reconstruction and geographic sampling could confound a correlation, and it does not discriminate why other herbivores in comparable settings did not reach sauropod sizes.

### D2 — Long-Neck Functional Threshold Test

Use three-dimensional body models to estimate feeding envelopes and mobility costs, then test whether a neck-to-trunk threshold predicts giant size. This is concrete and potentially informative, but it is **not selected** as a full answer: relative neck size is not tightly coupled to inferred feeding habits in all sauropod lineages, and a neck model alone leaves growth and pneumaticity unexplained.

### D3 — GigaCascade: Fossil-Anchored Causal Comparison of Constraint Release

Build an uncertainty-aware, time-calibrated comparative dataset that jointly tests feeding architecture, axial pneumaticity, life-history/growth proxies, and ecology. Use preregistered path models and leave-clade-out prediction to ask whether their interactions predict repeated giant mass shifts. This is **selected** because it answers the Survey's central gap: whether the cascade adds explanatory and predictive information beyond its individual parts.

### D4 — Dinosaur Metabolism Reconstruction from Bone Histology

Infer an energetic trajectory from histological features and test it against mass. This direction is high-risk because tissue microstructure is not a direct metabolic meter and could foster overconfident physiological claims. It is retained as an auxiliary sensitivity analysis, not a primary direction.

## Simulated evolutionary search trace

| Search node | Operation | Retained insight | Rejection rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed A | Reframe ``food access'' as intake opportunity | neck, small head and dentition must be separated | discard if only browse height is measured |
| Seed B | Reframe ``air sacs'' as a proxy bundle | compare structural, respiratory and thermal paths | discard direct metabolic conclusions |
| Seed C | Add life-history and phylogenetic timing | prerequisites must precede or co-emerge with mass shifts | discard purely cross-sectional correlation |
| D3.1 | Combine A+B+C under a causal graph | test interactions and mediation | require out-of-clade forecast |
| D3.2 | Add uncertainty and negative controls | protect against mass-estimate and sampling artifacts | require abstention on unidentifiable paths |

## Debate and selection

**Novelty challenge:** A new name for the established cascade would add no value. D3 is retained only because it converts the narrative into a registered comparison between single-factor, additive, interaction, and temporal-precedence models.

**Feasibility challenge:** Fossils do not yield direct energy budgets. D3 therefore treats energy balance as a latent, scenario-bounded construct and makes no claim to measure extinct oxygen consumption.

**Falsifiability challenge:** D3 fails if adding the proposed trait interactions does not improve prediction for held-out giant lineages, if the inferred prerequisites do not precede mass shifts, or if the effect is dominated by a small number of taxa or alternative mass estimates.

**Selection:** D3 is the primary direction. D1 and D2 are competitive ablations. D4 remains high-risk because it demands stronger validation than the fossil record currently supplies.
