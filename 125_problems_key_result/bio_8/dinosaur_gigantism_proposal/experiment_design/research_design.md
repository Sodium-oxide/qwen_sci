## Registered question

Does a preregistered interaction model of feeding architecture, axial pneumaticity, growth/life-history proxies, and ecological opportunity predict repeated giant body-mass evolution in sauropodomorphs better than single-factor or additive alternatives, after fossil and phylogenetic uncertainty are propagated?

## Study units and comparison design

The analysis unit is a taxon-time lineage represented by an operational taxonomic unit with a dated phylogenetic placement. The confirmatory panel will contain all sauropodomorph taxa meeting predeclared coverage thresholds for mass, time bin, and at least two non-mass trait blocks. The target is not fixed as a numerical sample size because availability and taxonomic independence must be audited before inclusion.

Three planned control groups prevent a narrative-only result:

1. **Non-giant sauropodomorphs:** test whether putative prerequisites occur without extreme mass.
2. **Large non-sauropod dinosaur herbivores:** test whether a giant outcome requires the same trait combination or follows a different route.
3. **Neutralized datasets:** phylogenetically constrained trait permutations and time-shuffled transitions test whether apparent fit follows the proposed causal order.

## Variables and evidence levels

| Block | Planned variables | Evidence status | Main error mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome | log body mass; giant-transition indicator; uncertainty interval | inferred from published scaling/volumetric estimates | dependence on reconstruction method |
| Feeding architecture | neck-to-trunk ratio; head proportion; dental replacement or oral-processing category; feeding-envelope proxy | morphology and dental proxy | reach is not realized intake |
| Pneumatic anatomy | axial pneumaticity extent; vertebral architecture; uncertainty category | osteological correlate | does not observe lung performance |
| Growth/life history | histological growth-state features; cortical tissue category; reproductive mode where defensible | proxy/inference | thin sampling and ontogenetic ambiguity |
| Ecology | paleoflora/facies proxy; latitude; formation/time-bin sampling effort | contextual proxy | preservation and collection bias |
| Structure | dated trees; taxon age range; formation identity | external model/input | tree and stratigraphic uncertainty |

## Analysis plan

1. Register candidate definitions, source hierarchy, trait coding rules, competing models, exclusions, and claim vocabulary before model fitting.
2. Draw posterior samples across mass estimates, trees, dates, and uncertain trait codings rather than selecting a single preferred reconstruction.
3. Fit phylogenetically structured models for (a) body-mass transition, (b) feeding proxy, and (c) causal-path alternatives. Evaluate direct paths, mediated paths, and preregistered interactions.
4. Fit a scenario-bounded feasibility calculation using intake opportunity, maintenance scaling, movement/load terms, and growth demand. It is an interpretive scaffold, not a measurement of dinosaur metabolism.
5. Compare against climate-only, neck-only, pneumaticity-only, growth-only, additive, and null models using out-of-clade prediction and predictive calibration.
6. Require temporal precedence/co-emergence checks: candidate enabling traits must not systematically appear only after the inferred mass transition.
7. Publish an abstention rather than a positive causal statement where alternative reconstructions dominate the posterior or the required evidence block is missing.

## Decision rules

**Conditional support** requires all of the following: predictive improvement over preregistered alternatives; directionally stable effects across sensitivity panels; no single taxon/clade dominance; plausible trait timing; and explicitly bounded proxy interpretation.

**Partial support** permits only an association statement when predictive improvement occurs without resolved temporal order or mediator identification.

**ABSTAIN** is required when model rankings change under plausible mass/phylogeny inputs, missingness clusters by clade or formation, or a proxy is being asked to identify unobserved respiratory or metabolic performance.

## Governance and preservation safeguards

The confirmatory analysis uses published measurements, published scans, and curator-approved non-destructive specimen records where available. No destructive sampling, CT scanning, specimen transport, or museum collection intervention is authorized by this design. Any future new data collection requires curator approval, locality/legal compliance, explicit sampling minimization, and a separate specimen-level protocol.
