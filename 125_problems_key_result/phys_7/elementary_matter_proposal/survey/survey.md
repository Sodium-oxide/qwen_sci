# Survey Agent: The Smallest Building Blocks of Matter

## Scientific reframing

The phrase "smallest building blocks" is operational rather than metaphysical. Within the Standard Model (SM), quarks and leptons are treated as elementary fermion fields, and gauge bosons plus the Higgs boson are elementary bosonic fields. Protons and neutrons are not elementary: they are composite hadrons whose quark and gluon dynamics are described by quantum chromodynamics. Current scattering evidence supports pointlike behavior of known quarks and leptons over the tested momentum-transfer range, but it cannot prove that no substructure exists at every higher scale.

This proposal asks a narrower, testable question:

> Can a momentum-transfer-matched, cross-channel inference program distinguish a pointlike-SM explanation from finite-size, contact-interaction, or excited-fermion alternatives while controlling effective-field-theory (EFT) validity, parton-distribution uncertainty, detector response, and channel-specific systematics?

The scope corrects three common simplifications. First, a boson is not automatically a force carrier: gauge bosons mediate gauge interactions, whereas the Higgs boson is a scalar excitation associated with electroweak symmetry breaking. Second, quarks are not observed as free particles because of confinement; evidence for their pointlike behavior is indirect through high-energy scattering and hadronic structure. Third, an absence of a deviation supplies a range-dependent constraint, not a proof of absolute indivisibility.

## Evidence-bounded foundations

The SM is a gauge quantum field theory with a matter sector of quarks and leptons and a bosonic sector. The six quark flavors and six leptons are organized in three generations. Gauge bosons are associated with the strong, weak, and electromagnetic gauge interactions; the Higgs field supplies a distinct scalar sector. This classification is experimentally successful, but it does not include a quantum description of gravity and does not by itself explain every observed cosmological or flavor fact \cite{pdg2008,cottingham2007}.

At experimental resolution, pointlike behavior is tested through scattering amplitudes, angular distributions, form factors, contact interactions, and searches for excited states. In a low-energy representation of a heavy new interaction, a representative deformation is L_eff = L_SM + sum_i (C_i / Lambda^2) O_i + .... The notation does not identify a constituent. It parameterizes possible deviations below a declared cutoff. High-energy Drell--Yan tails are useful because they can constrain semileptonic interactions, but conclusions depend on EFT order, flavor structure, and validity regime \cite{allwicher2023}. The SMEFTsim framework similarly shows that choices of basis, electroweak input scheme, and flavor assumptions enter a global analysis \cite{brivio2017}.

An explicit excited-lepton search provides a complementary target. A null result in one final state constrains the benchmark it tests, rather than demonstrating that every lepton is elementary at all scales \cite{cms_tau2025}. Future high-energy and lepton-hadron programs can extend or complement the resolution ladder, but projections must not be converted into data claims \cite{arrington2021,abada2019}.

## Survey conclusion

The best-supported answer is conditional: quarks, charged leptons, neutrinos, gauge bosons, and the Higgs boson are the current elementary fields of the Standard Model, while protons and neutrons are composite. The experimentally meaningful frontier is not to announce that a final smallest scale has been proven, but to improve the resolution, model discrimination, and uncertainty accounting that would expose finite size, contact interactions, excited states, or other departures. This conclusion motivates a cross-channel compositeness test that treats negative and ambiguous outcomes as scientifically informative.
