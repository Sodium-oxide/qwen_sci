# Idea Agent: Coherence-Resolved Instability Suppression Protocol for ICF

## Candidate portfolio

1. **Bandwidth-only threshold map.** Vary relative bandwidth against a Raman/Brillouin proxy. This is useful but rejected as a primary direction because it treats polarization, temporal correlation, and multi-beam overlap as nuisance variables even though the Survey evidence makes them mechanistically relevant.
2. **Polarization-only control dashboard.** Compare fixed, orthogonal, rotating, and vector polarization. This retains fast-time smoothing logic but is incomplete because it cannot decide when stochastic spectral structure changes resonance memory.
3. **CRISP-ICF: Coherence-Resolved Instability Suppression Protocol.** A source-bounded, multi-fidelity design that represents each candidate driver by a coherence descriptor; compares it against matched coherent, SSD-like, and polarization-smoothing controls; then propagates only qualifying candidates through coupling and drive-compatibility gates.

## Selected primary idea

**CRISP-ICF** is selected because it binds every claimed benefit to a declared quantity of interest and makes adverse outcomes first-class. Its unit of analysis is:

`physical scope x driver coherence card x propagation/coupling card x declared quantity of interest`.

The protocol asks whether a candidate that combines bandwidth, random phase, and random polarization creates a *conditional evidence case* for lower LPI risk without degrading a declared drive or coupling proxy. It never turns an unrun model into an ignition statement.

### Core hypothesis

For a fixed, explicitly bounded plasma and beam geometry, a driver whose temporal coherence, spectral support, and polarization statistics decorrelate three-wave resonance on a timescale comparable with the instability response can produce a more favorable multi-objective LPI ledger than a matched coherent baseline. The hypothesis fails if the apparent reduction disappears when spectrum, energy, focal statistics, overlap, or model fidelity are matched; if it is offset by unacceptable drive/coupling penalties; or if the conclusion changes under reasonable representation choices.

### Why it is more than a writing concept

- It turns the term *sunlight-like* into measurable design descriptors: relative bandwidth, spectral shape, phase-correlation time, polarization Stokes-statistics, temporal-speckle lifetime, spatial-intensity statistics, and inter-beam independence.
- It forces comparison with credible controls rather than a single monochromatic baseline.
- It contains an adversarial overlapping-beam card because high-intensity speckles and cross-beam effects can reverse a favorable single-beam narrative.
- It separates analytic screening, wave/kinetic simulation planning, and target-scale inference, preventing the transfer of a reduced-model result without an evidence bridge.

### Portfolio result

| Class | Direction | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | CRISP-ICF multi-objective coherence ledger | Selected: aligns with all accepted Survey gaps. |
| Competitive | Polarization-control-only route | Retained as a component bridge and control condition. |
| High risk | End-to-end sunlight-like ignition optimizer | Retained as future work only; currently lacks a validated transfer and hardware contract. |
| Rejected | Universal bandwidth score | Rejected: no quantity of interest, no regime scope, and no overlap/coupling control. |

## Idea evolution trace

`root: sunlight-like driver` -> `defect: metaphor lacks measurable descriptor` -> `descriptor: coherence vector` -> `defect: suppression can create tradeoffs` -> `ledger: LPI plus delivery/coupling objectives` -> `defect: reduced model does not transfer` -> `CRISP-ICF: staged fidelity and falsification gates`.
