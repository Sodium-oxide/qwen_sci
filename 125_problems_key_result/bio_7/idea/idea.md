# Idea: MAGNAV - A Multisensory Animal GeoNavigation Assessment and Validation Framework

## Core idea

MAGNAV is a design-only framework for testing how a specified migratory animal uses available sensory information to produce route-relevant behaviour. It rejects the false choice between a single "magnetic sense" and a purely learned landscape map. Instead, it asks what cue is used, when it is weighted, what computation it supports, how it interacts with other cues, and whether its contribution survives ecologically relevant conditions.

## Claim ladder

The framework distinguishes five claims that often become conflated:

1. **Cue availability:** the animal can physically access a visual, celestial, olfactory, hydrodynamic, or magnetic signal.
2. **Cue-dependent orientation:** controlled rotation, displacement, masking, or removal changes a preregistered bearing or choice.
3. **Mechanistic mediation:** a candidate receptor or neural pathway is necessary or sufficient within a stated assay and context.
4. **Map or route contribution:** the manipulation changes position correction, route choice, stopover selection, or return accuracy in an ecologically relevant setting.
5. **Generalization:** independent work establishes comparable function across a clearly delimited taxon and condition set.

Evidence at one level cannot substitute for evidence at a later level. For example, magnetic-field-sensitive CRY4 photochemistry is compatible with a receptor hypothesis, but does not establish a free-flying bird's map, nor does a successful navigation track identify CRY4 as its cause.

## Hypotheses

- **H0 - apparent reliance:** apparent navigation changes arise from uncontrolled light, handling, arena, wind, release-site, or selection effects rather than the nominated cue.
- **H1 - conditional cue integration:** a defined cue changes bearing or route decisions only in a prespecified sensory and migratory context, with a predicted restoration effect.
- **H2 - mechanism-to-behaviour bridge:** a defined receptor/pathway perturbation changes the response to the relevant cue while leaving matched non-target controls interpretable.
- **H3 - context or lineage restriction:** the cue or mechanism contributes only to a subset of species, stages, light regimes, geomagnetic locations, or route segments.

## Innovation

The primary innovation is a **cue-dependence ledger**, not a universal navigation score. Every result records: (a) the manipulated cue; (b) cues retained; (c) the predicted response; (d) the population and route boundary; (e) competing explanations; (f) restoration and null controls; and (g) the strongest conclusion permitted. MAGNAV also joins laboratory and field evidence through a predeclared scenario ladder.

- `S0` - species, route, and sensory-context atlas;
- `S1` - controlled compass and cue-conflict tests;
- `S2` - receptor, neural, and computational mechanism tests;
- `S3` - free-ranging track and virtual-displacement validation;
- `S4` - independent replication and cross-context extension.

## Decision rule

Advancement requires noncompensatory gates: a statistically detectable bearing shift cannot overcome a failed handling control, a missing restoration response, a confounded light spectrum, a biologically implausible manipulation, or a field track with no location or exposure provenance. MAGNAV therefore turns the broad question "How do animals know?" into a sequence of smaller questions that can be answered, contradicted, or localized.
