# Survey: Can Humans Hibernate?

## Scientific reframing
This proposal asks whether a reversible, controlled, low-metabolic state can be induced and safely reversed in humans. It does not equate cooling with hibernation and rejects the claim that a cooled human would need no fuel. Natural torpor and hibernation are actively regulated whole-organism programs: metabolism, thermoregulation, circulation, ventilation, substrate use, and arousal are coordinated. Lower body temperature reduces metabolic demand, but vital cells still require energy and controlled rewarming creates additional risk.

## Evidence boundary
Natural hibernators can substantially lower metabolic rate and body temperature, but the degree and control architecture vary across species [S1,S2]. Human therapeutic hypothermia is a time-limited clinical intervention, not a sustained hibernation program; its physiology includes arrhythmia, coagulopathy, infection, electrolyte shifts, shivering, and rewarming hazards [S3,S4]. Neural-circuit studies in rodents show that hibernation-like or torpor-like states can be experimentally engaged, but their translation to conscious, nonhibernating humans is unproven [S5].

## Accepted gaps
**GAP-HT-01:** There is no validated human protocol that jointly controls heat balance, metabolic substrate supply, shivering suppression, perfusion, immune risk, and rewarming for prolonged reversible torpor. **GAP-HT-02:** No out-of-sample safety-and-reversibility framework separates an energy-saving state from dangerous hypothermia in humans.
