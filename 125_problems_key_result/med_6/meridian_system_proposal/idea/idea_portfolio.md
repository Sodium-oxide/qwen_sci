# Idea Agent Portfolio: From “Does a Meridian Exist?” to a Testable Research Program

## Reframed problem

The traditional question is usually framed as a search for a hidden structure. That framing rewards post-hoc resemblance: nearly any long anatomical plane, vascular path, neural distribution, or tracer pattern can be compared visually with a drawn channel. The Survey Agent instead requires a **residual-specificity question**:

> After explicit control of known anatomy, tissue composition, stimulation characteristics, expectancy, and spatial autocorrelation, does the canonical meridian assignment provide reproducible predictive information?

This is a stronger scientific test because it can produce a positive result, a bounded negative result, or an explicit abstention when measurement coverage is insufficient.

## Candidate directions

| Direction | Core intervention | Scientific value | Primary weakness | Portfolio decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1: high-resolution anatomical atlas | Map canonical trajectories against fascia, nerves, vessels, and interstitial spaces. | Clarifies correspondence claims. | An atlas alone cannot distinguish a named channel from ubiquitous anatomy. | Competitive supporting direction. |
| D2: Meridian Residual Specificity Trial (MRST) | Pre-register canonical, anatomy-matched, shifted-path, and sham-location comparisons across multimodal measurements. | Directly tests whether the label adds explanatory value beyond known anatomy. | Requires careful spatial control, blinding, and calibration. | **Selected primary direction.** |

## Selected primary idea — D2

**Title:** *Meridian Residual Specificity Trial: A Pre-Registered Multimodal Test of Canonical Trajectories Against Matched Anatomical Controls*

### Root hypothesis

If the Meridian System has an independently testable physical or physiological basis, canonical trajectory membership should explain a stable, replicated residual signal after known anatomical covariates and measurement artifacts have been modeled. If canonical labels do not add information once those covariates are controlled, the sampled phenomenon is more parsimoniously explained by ordinary anatomy and generic somatosensory physiology.

### Scientific object and intervention

The object is not “qi” as an assumed measurable substance. It is a set of declared, historical canonical trajectories represented as blinded coordinate polylines. The intervention is a future, ethics-approved measurement protocol that samples these coordinates and four comparator classes:

1. **Canonical path coordinates** defined before data collection.
2. **Anatomy-matched noncanonical coordinates** with comparable fascial plane, tissue depth, and regional innervation.
3. **Spatially shifted controls** preserving body region and measurement sequence but not the canonical label.
4. **Negative technical controls** used to quantify electrode, ultrasound, and segmentation drift.

### Predicted observable

A meridian-specific account predicts a reproducible canonical-label effect that survives:

- ultrasound-derived fascial-plane and collagen-band descriptors;
- skin and subcutaneous impedance calibration;
- local vascular and temperature measures where feasible;
- tissue depth, hydration-related proxy, and electrode/needle contact quality;
- regional nerve-density or sensory-threshold proxies;
- participant expectation, operator identity, and spatial autocorrelation;
- pre-specified multiplicity correction and held-out trajectory replication.

### Falsifiable conditions

D2 is weakened if any of the following holds:

- canonical versus matched-control differences disappear after anatomy and acquisition covariates are included;
- the direction or magnitude reverses across trajectories or laboratories;
- a spatially shifted control predicts the signal as well as the canonical label;
- predictive performance cannot transfer to a held-out trajectory;
- results can be reproduced by generic local stimulation without canonical location information.

### Why D2 is preferable

D1 can find visually suggestive overlaps but cannot rule out ubiquity of fascia. D3 is important but clinically and statistically underdetermined for an ontological claim. D2 joins their strengths: it is data-generating, preregisterable, and specifically designed to separate an anatomy-correspondence hypothesis from a distinct-channel hypothesis. It makes a strong claim only when a stronger control architecture has been passed.

## Debate record

**Advocate:** The repeated use of canonical paths across historical practice justifies testing their predictive value rather than dismissing them because no single gross anatomical structure has been accepted.

**Skeptic:** A historical coordinate map is not an independent biological variable if its locations systematically follow accessible intermuscular planes, neurovascular bundles, or regions selected for sensory salience.

**Resolution:** Both positions predict different outcomes under anatomy-matched, spatially shifted controls. The selected design therefore treats canonical labels as a testable annotation, not as a premise.

## Nonnegotiable safeguards

- D2 produces a research protocol only; it does not recruit participants, administer treatment, or give medical advice.
- No result may be described as proof of qi, universal organ correspondence, or clinical efficacy without an appropriate independent study.
- The 2020 physiological-spaces article remains a hypothesis-generating source, not an outcome benchmark.
- A negative result is informative and must be published with the same transparency as a positive result.
