# Survey: future human physical phenotypes

## Operational problem

The question "Will humans look physically different in the future?" cannot
be answered scientifically by drawing a universal future face or body. An
observed difference between cohorts can arise from several non-equivalent
processes: genetic evolution over generations, developmental responses to
conditions during ontogeny, medical or cosmetic interventions, migration and
population composition, or changes in how a phenotype is measured. The
scientific task is therefore pathway attribution, not appearance prophecy.

This Survey asks which of those pathways can be measured separately for
pre-specified traits, whether their contribution can be distinguished from
sampling artifacts, and what evidence would justify a conditional forecast.

## Frozen evidence

### Developmental plasticity

Ponzi et al. reviewed human hormonal developmental plasticity and describe
phenotypic change during ontogeny as mediated by endocrine systems that are
responsive to developmental conditions. This supports the premise that
cohort-level bodily differences need not be inherited genetic change. It does
not identify a universal direction of change for a particular phenotype.

### Third-molar agenesis is a useful, but limited, dental example

Carter et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of third-molar
agenesis. The pooled worldwide rate was 22.63 percent, with a 95 percent
confidence interval of 20.64 to 24.76 percent, and estimates varied from
5.32 to 56.0 percent among the contributing studies. The study supports
heterogeneity by geography and demographic variables. It does not demonstrate
that the worldwide frequency is currently increasing, that reduced jaw size
causes agenesis, or that dietary change has selected a gene for tooth loss.
Dental care, impaction, eruption, extraction, and agenesis are separate
outcomes and must remain separate in the protocol.

### Culture can alter selection environments but is not a shortcut to a
genetic forecast

Laland et al. review gene-culture coevolution and explain why culturally
created environments can interact with genetic evolution. This is a
mechanistic reason to model technology and diet as exposures and to consider
long time scales. It is not evidence that any current technology will produce
a given inherited physical trait.

### Polygenic population claims require strong control of structure

Berg et al. compared signals of polygenic height adaptation using UK Biobank
effect estimates with prior analyses. They reported strongly attenuated or
absent signals and evidence that earlier analyses were confounded by
population stratification. Consequently, differences in polygenic scores
between populations are not a valid shortcut to a forecast of future
appearance. The 1000 Genomes reference supports the practical need to
document genotype processing and population structure, but it does not
license biological typologies.

### Prediction studies need explicit reporting and bias assessment

The TRIPOD statement requires transparent reporting for development and
validation of multivariable prediction models. PROBAST organizes bias and
applicability review around participants, predictors, outcomes, and analysis.
These sources motivate the design's locked endpoint, external validation,
calibration checks, and release gates. They do not make a planned model
clinically valid.

## Claim boundaries

1. Evidence supports multiple pathways to population-level phenotype
   difference. It does not support a single universal appearance trajectory.
2. Third-molar agenesis is a measurable congenital developmental phenotype,
   while tooth eruption, impaction, extraction, and orthodontic treatment are
   different variables.
3. Any genetic contribution is an optional, carefully controlled part of a
   population analysis. It must not be interpreted as deterministic,
   individual, or racial prediction.
4. A cohort trend without harmonized measurements and demographic adjustment
   is insufficient to infer either environmental causality or selection.

## Research gaps accepted for Idea

- GAP-FP-01: There is no standard longitudinal framework that jointly models
  genetic, developmental, technological, demographic, and measurement
  pathways before describing a physical phenotype as "evolving."
- GAP-FP-02: Dental and anthropometric cohorts often do not distinguish
  congenital presence or absence from post-developmental care and treatment
  histories when communicating population trends.
- GAP-FP-03: Forecast-oriented phenotype models rarely treat temporal
  transport, site transport, calibration, and population-structure sensitivity
  as mandatory gates rather than optional analyses.

## Survey conclusion

The defensible research direction is a preregistered population-level
Phenotype Pathway Attribution Atlas. It tests whether a pathway-aware model
explains and predicts selected, carefully measured cohort differences better
than an age-and-site baseline. It does not predict individual appearance or
make an irreversible claim about future humans.
