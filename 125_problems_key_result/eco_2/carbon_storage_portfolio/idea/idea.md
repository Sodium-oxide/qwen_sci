# Idea Agent: PERSIST-CO2

## Problem reconstruction

The Survey Agent shows that “where to put CO2” is a portfolio and accounting problem. A sink is useful only if it removes additional atmospheric CO2, keeps it retained for a declared horizon, can be monitored, and does not create larger lifecycle or social burdens elsewhere. The Idea Agent therefore searches for a policy and infrastructure design rather than a single storage technology.

## Candidate routes

1. **Geology-first:** route captured CO2 to saline formations or depleted reservoirs and optimize injection volume.
2. **Biology-first:** expand forests, soils, wood products, wetlands, and seagrass as the main sink.
3. **Engineered-removal-first:** scale DACCS, BECCS, mineralization, enhanced weathering, and ocean methods according to nominal cost.
4. **Avoidance-only:** prioritize gross emissions reduction and treat storage as unnecessary.
5. **PERSIST-CO2:** choose a diversified portfolio under durability-adjusted lifecycle, resource, monitoring, equity, and stewardship constraints.

## Primary direction

The selected direction is **PERSIST-CO2 (Permanence, Energy, Reversal, Storage Integrity, and Trade-offs for CO2)**. Its central hypothesis is:

> A permanence-aware portfolio that combines durable geological or mineral storage with appropriately bounded biological and engineered removals, and funds monitoring as a capacity, will achieve lower worst-case climate benefit regret than any single-pathway or nominal-cost strategy under plausible energy, land, water, leakage, and reversal futures.

The design has four linked layers:

- **carbon accounting:** gross capture, lifecycle emissions, transport, leakage, reversal, and net atmospheric removal;
- **reservoir dynamics:** pathway-specific retention and failure processes across years to centuries;
- **resource and infrastructure:** energy, land, water, minerals, pipelines, wells, monitoring, and stewardship capacity;
- **portfolio governance:** additionality tests, staged deployment, liability reserves, monitoring triggers, and transparent retirement of invalid credits.

## Why this idea wins

Geological storage offers the strongest durability candidate but is site-specific and infrastructure-intensive. Biological storage can deliver valuable co-benefits but is exposed to reversal and land competition. Engineered removal can address dispersed atmospheric CO2 but is energy- and materials-intensive. PERSIST-CO2 keeps those differences visible and asks where each pathway belongs in a bounded portfolio.

## Falsifiable predictions

1. The robust portfolio will not maximize nominal tonnes captured by one pathway.
2. Durability-adjusted accounting will reduce the apparent advantage of reversible biological storage in high-disturbance scenarios.
3. Monitoring and verification capacity will become a binding constraint before nominal pore volume or biomass area is exhausted in some scenarios.
4. A mixed portfolio with early emissions avoidance, durable storage, bounded biological removal, and staged engineered deployment will have lower worst-case regret than a single-pathway strategy.

The ExperimentDesign Agent must convert these predictions into a common mass balance, pathway-specific retention models, infrastructure constraints, controls, and falsification criteria. It must preserve the design-only boundary.
