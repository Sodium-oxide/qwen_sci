# Idea Agent: CO-FET

## Problem reconstruction

The Survey handoff shows that the bottleneck is not a missing climate forecast in isolation. It is the missing interface between observations, uncertainty, physical risk, energy-system investment, and accountable action. The Idea Agent therefore treats a policy portfolio as the research object and asks which portfolio remains useful across plausible futures.

## Search space

Four routes were considered:

1. **Observation-first:** expand climate monitoring and improve forecasts, then defer energy decisions.
2. **Technology-only:** minimize the cost of a renewable and storage build-out without a coupled risk or data-value layer.
3. **Carbon-removal-first:** use future negative emissions as the principal route while delaying near-term structural mitigation.
4. **CO-FET:** jointly evaluate observations, climate risk, clean-energy portfolios, grid flexibility, equity, and staged triggers.

## Primary direction

The selected direction is **CO-FET (Climate Observing and Flexible Energy Transition)**. Its core hypothesis is:

> A coordinated policy that jointly values emissions reduction, climate-risk reduction, grid reliability, affordability, equity, and observation quality will have lower worst-case regret than carbon-only, technology-only, observation-first, or delayed-action policies across held-out climate and socioeconomic futures.

CO-FET has four coupled layers:

- an observation layer with coverage, calibration, uncertainty, continuity, openness, and latency;
- a risk layer for hazards, exposure, vulnerability, adaptation, and sectoral loss;
- an energy layer for generation, transmission, storage, reserves, demand response, and curtailment;
- a policy layer for robust milestones, investment triggers, review ownership, and rollback options.

## Why this idea wins

It directly addresses all five high-priority gaps in the ledger. It gives observations a measurable value-of-information target, turns grid flexibility into a first-class mitigation variable, reports regional and access-group outcomes, and makes uncertainty operational through staged triggers. It is ambitious enough to test a real data-to-decision proposition while remaining falsifiable: the coupled framework can be rejected if simpler policies perform as well or better under the same constraints and held-out scenarios.

## Falsifiable predictions

1. Robust portfolios will combine efficiency, clean generation, transmission, storage, demand response, and selected firm low-carbon resources rather than rely on one technology.
2. Observation upgrades will be valuable when they change an investment or risk trigger and reduce regret, not merely when they increase data volume.
3. Policies optimized only for mean emissions will produce more reliability or distributional failures in some stress scenarios.
4. Staged policies with hysteresis and pre-declared review points will outperform delayed action when uncertainty is material, while avoiding unnecessary switching when observations remain within the envelope.

The next agent must convert these predictions into a scenario, optimization, and ablation design. It may not turn them into observed results.
