# Evidence-Constrained Survey

## Scientific reframing

The broad question “Can we stop global climate change?” is converted into a decision-relevant question:

> Can a coordinated data-to-decision system identify mitigation portfolios that drive anthropogenic greenhouse-gas emissions toward net zero, maintain reliable and affordable energy, and reduce residual climate risk across plausible climate and socioeconomic futures?

The phrase *stop climate change* is therefore operationalized as stopping the continued growth of anthropogenic forcing through net-zero greenhouse-gas emissions, while separately measuring unavoidable warming, physical impacts, adaptation limits, and distributional burdens. The target is not a claim that the climate can be returned instantly to a historical state.

## Evidence map

### 1. Climate observations are enabling infrastructure

The Global Climate Observing System (GCOS) identifies sustained and quality-controlled observations as the basis for monitoring, understanding, prediction, climate services, and adaptation. Essential Climate Variables provide a common organizing layer across atmosphere, ocean, land, cryosphere, and related domains. The relevant research problem is not merely collecting more data: it is preserving calibration, uncertainty, continuity, interoperability, access, and decision-relevant latency.

### 2. Mitigation is a system transition

The International Energy Agency’s Net Zero by 2050 roadmap describes a pathway requiring rapid deployment of clean electricity, efficiency, electrification, innovation, and international cooperation. The pathway is portfolio-shaped: renewable generation, storage, transmission, demand response, firm low-carbon resources, sustainable fuels, and carbon management have different constraints and risks.

The IEA assessment of electricity grids identifies grid infrastructure, planning, connection queues, and system operation as potential bottlenecks. Installed renewable capacity is therefore not equivalent to delivered low-carbon energy. A credible design must represent generation, transmission, storage, reserve, curtailment, outages, and flexible demand together.

### 3. Risk is coupled and unequal

The IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report and Working Group II assessment treat climate risk as an interaction among hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and adaptation capacity. Risks span health, ecosystems, food, water, coasts, infrastructure, and human settlements. A global emissions objective can coexist with local energy poverty, water stress, land impacts, or concentrated climate harm; the study must report distributional outcomes instead of only a global mean.

### 4. Decision-making occurs before uncertainty is resolved

Climate sensitivity, extreme-event frequency, technology costs, demand, renewable availability, storage duration, transmission build-out, and governance capacity remain uncertain. The actionable question is consequently one of robust and adaptive decisions: which early actions are useful across futures, which investments preserve options, and which observations change decisions enough to justify their cost?

## Gap ledger

1. Observation quality is rarely evaluated by the decisions it changes.
2. Energy-transition studies can treat grid flexibility as an implementation detail instead of a coupled resource.
3. Global carbon objectives can hide regional and social differences in residual risk and energy access.
4. Policies must be staged before key uncertainties resolve, but trigger rules and rollback conditions are under-specified.
5. Cross-country and cross-sector coordination lacks a shared interface for assumptions, provenance, uncertainty, and accountability.

## Boundary conditions

The survey supports a research design that couples observation, risk, energy-system flexibility, and staged governance. It does not support claims that better prediction alone stops warming, that one technology is universally sufficient, or that carbon removal can be credited without accounting for permanence, land, water, and monitoring. It also does not justify a claim that a proposed portfolio has already reduced emissions: the downstream study is design-only until it is executed with declared data and validated models.

## Candidate research direction

The strongest evidence-compatible direction is **CO-FET (Climate Observing and Flexible Energy Transition)**: a scenario and optimization framework that assigns value to observations through their effect on mitigation and risk decisions, while evaluating low-carbon portfolios under grid reliability, affordability, equity, and climate-risk constraints. The Idea Agent receives this direction together with the five gaps, the evidence boundaries, and the verified source registry.
