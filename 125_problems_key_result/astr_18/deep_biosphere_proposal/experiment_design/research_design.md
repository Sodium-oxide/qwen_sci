# ExperimentDesign Agent: PRESSURE-ATLAS Study Design

## Boundary and research brief

The design is DESIGN_ONLY. It proposes a future observational and analytical program; it reports no cruise, sample, assay, genomic sequence, rate, or observed result.

PRESSURE-ATLAS estimates four linked objects: accessible physical habitat volume, absolute cell inventory, pressure-preserved community composition, and carbon-linked metabolic flux. The unit of inference is a compartment-by-stratum cell: water mass, sediment depth and lithology, or crustal-fluid setting crossed with pressure, temperature, redox state, organic-carbon supply, and geographic province.

## Sampling and measurements

The design uses paired pressure-retained and decompressed aliquots where logistics permit. It includes field blanks, extraction blanks, mock-community controls, replicate cores or water collections, and independent geochemical checks. Absolute abundance combines microscopy or cytometry with marker-gene calibration. Composition combines 16S rRNA profiling and metagenomics; activity is assessed with pressure-compatible isotope or substrate-turnover measurements and transcript or protein evidence only as corroboration.

Physical volume is estimated from bathymetry, sediment thickness and porosity, and crustal circulation constraints. The study never multiplies volume by a global average cell number without stratification. A hierarchical model propagates sampling error, detection limits, blank contamination, pressure response, and unobserved strata into intervals rather than a single artificial total.

## Decision rules

1. A reported taxon is a composition result; it is not called metabolically active without direct rate or labeled-substrate evidence.
2. A process rate measured after decompression is not used as in situ flux without a pressure correction established from paired observations.
3. Global aggregation is released only if posterior predictive checks agree with withheld geochemical observations in each compartment.
4. A mismatch is not discarded: it becomes evidence that the model or compartment definition is incomplete.

## Human and environmental safeguards

Sampling requires marine permits, shipboard contamination control, pressure-vessel safety procedures, and site-specific environmental review. The design does not authorize autonomous field deployment or wet-lab execution. Human experts must approve preservation, pressure handling, isotope use, and any interpretation relevant to resource exploration or astrobiology.
