\section{Theory-to-Observation Bridge}

\subsection{A hierarchy of claims}

The phrase ``the arrow of time'' can name claims with very different evidentiary status. At the strongest empirical level, a controlled finite system can display a time-asymmetric trajectory distribution that is consistent with a specified entropy-production ledger. At an intermediate descriptive level, an observer can have stable records and predictive variables even though the complete thermodynamic ledger is inaccessible. At a cosmological level, a model can make its low-entropy boundary condition explicit and investigate the consequences of that assumption. These levels should communicate, but none should be promoted to another level merely because they share the word entropy.

RAAT uses this hierarchy to prevent a common inferential error. Suppose that a process produces a large positive value of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mean_asymmetry}. That value is direct evidence only for the asymmetry of the modeled forward and reverse path distributions. To call it physical dissipation, the state variables, bath, work protocol, and reversal operation must be justified. To connect it to a record, a persistence criterion and a system-environment split must be provided. To connect it to cosmology, an additional initial-condition and gravity model must be supplied. The proposed workflow records every transition in this chain as an assumption-bearing interface.

This does not make the explanation weak. It makes it discriminating. A thermodynamic account that survives the interfaces identifies the circumstances in which macroscopic irreversibility is more than a visual asymmetry. A record account that survives its interface identifies why a finite observer has useful temporal correlations. A cosmological account that states its boundary condition identifies the exact explanatory burden that remains. Separating these achievements makes it possible for future evidence to strengthen one link without claiming to have settled all links.

\subsection{Protocol symmetry and causal alternatives}

Time reversal in a physical model is not equivalent to reading a stored sequence backwards. Velocities, magnetic fields, externally driven controls, and measurement operations can have different parity or protocol transformation rules. A benchmark that reverses an array while leaving a time-dependent drive unchanged can create an artificial directional contrast. RAAT therefore makes protocol symmetry a first-class object. For every planned forward condition, the design specifies how $\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}$ changes the drive, which state variables are odd under reversal, and which data transformations are merely display operations.

The same distinction matters for causality. A record correlated with an earlier state does not by itself prove that the earlier state caused the record under every intervention. In the proposed benchmark, causal language is reserved for generator-level interventions: changing an affinity, changing a bath parameter, hiding a state, or introducing a measurement asymmetry. Each intervention has a predeclared alternative explanation and a response pattern. The analysis then asks whether the observed temporal score changes in the direction predicted by the model card and whether an artifact injection can reproduce that change. This is stricter than narrating a direction from an unmanipulated series.

For a candidate model $M$, the proposed cross-resolution stability functional is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(M)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{valid}}}w_r\,\mathbf{1}\!\left[\operatorname{sign}(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_r)=\operatorname{sign}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{r,\mathrm{ledger}})\right]-\lambda\,\overline{D}_r,
\label{eq:stability_functional}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{valid}}$ is the prespecified valid portion of the observation ladder, $w_r$ are declared resolution weights, $\overline{D}_r$ is the planned aggregate discrepancy from Eq.~\eqref{eq:discrepancy}, and $\lambda$ is fixed before analysis. Equation~\eqref{eq:stability_functional} is not a universal entropy measure. It is a transparent scoring aid for deciding whether a particular attribution is stable enough to merit the stronger conclusion branch.

\subsection{Records, memory, and the direction of inference}

The familiar subjective sense that the past is fixed while the future is open also has an information-theoretic component. Records, traces, and memories are present variables correlated with selected earlier variables; they are physical systems and therefore carry costs, noise, and a finite lifetime. Decoherence can make such records robust by dispersing phase information into an environment \cite{zurek1993}. Coarse graining can make the resulting description predictable by suppressing inaccessible detail \cite{gellmann_hartle2007}. Neither observation establishes that an observer's access to records is identical to the total entropy increase of a closed universe.

RAAT operationalizes this distinction with a planned record-persistence statistic. A record variable is eligible only if its correlation with a declared target remains above a prespecified threshold across a fixed forecast horizon and if the noise model is documented. The statistic is reported next to, not inside, the entropy ledger. This architecture allows several informative patterns. A driven system may exhibit both record persistence and dissipative support. A partially observed system may retain directional records while receiving only a descriptive-asymmetry classification. An equilibrium negative control may produce transient correlations without supporting a persistent net-dissipation attribution. These distinctions offer a more precise answer to the popular question than an all-purpose slogan about disorder.

\subsection{Reproducibility as an explanatory control}

The benchmark's final bridge is reproducibility. In foundational work, an undocumented reversal convention or an unrecorded aggregation choice can change the apparent philosophical conclusion. RAAT therefore treats artifacts as part of the evidence: generator equations, parameter cards, initial-state distributions, observation maps, reversal functions, seeds, inference versions, and outcome-class decisions must be retained together. The same principle constrains use of cosmological analogies. A toy low-gravitational-entropy proxy must list its gravity assumptions and may not be rendered in a plot with the visual authority of an observed cosmological quantity.

This documentation policy is also a route to productive disagreement. Different researchers may favor different coarse grainings, quantum partitions, or gravitational-entropy proposals. Rather than forcing agreement through a single score, the benchmark exposes how the conclusion changes under each declared choice. A result that is stable across legitimate alternatives earns broader support. A result that is not stable identifies the missing physics or measurement needed for resolution. In both cases, the research program turns a diffuse debate about time into a set of transparent, falsifiable interfaces.
