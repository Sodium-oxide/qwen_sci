# Why Does Life Require Chirality? A Design-Only Four-Agent Research Proposal

## Abstract

Terrestrial biochemistry is not merely composed of arbitrary ``right-handed'' molecules. It is organized around compatible stereochemical conventions: proteins predominantly contain L-amino acids, while the sugars of DNA and RNA have the D configuration. This report asks what follows from that observation. The evidence supports a strong conditional statement: for familiar DNA/RNA/protein-like systems, compatible stereochemistry supports template copying, reproducible folding, molecular recognition, and selective catalysis. It does not yet support the universal statement that every possible system worthy of the name life must use one terrestrial hand or even a simple same-handed rule. This design-only proposal therefore tests *stereochemical closure* rather than a preferred hand. Closure means that the relations among monomers, templates, catalysts, and recognition partners remain compatible enough to preserve heredity and functional organization.

## Survey result

The Survey Agent distinguishes molecular chirality, biological homochirality, and universal necessity. The primary RNA literature shows why the distinction has functional weight: template-directed reactions are much more effective for matching stereochemical inputs, while mixed inputs can inhibit extension. Reviews and a prebiotic RNA-precursor crystallization study make several routes to enantioenrichment plausible, but do not identify a unique historical origin mechanism. A cross-chiral RNA polymerase ribozyme is a crucial boundary case: it demonstrates a workable complementary relation across opposite configurations in a limited molecular setting. It neither creates a mirror organism nor establishes achiral life.

The evidence gaps are: an unjustified jump from terrestrial function to universal law; weak linkage among enrichment, polymer function, and evolvability; under-formalized treatment of cross-chiral alternatives; and a failure to declare which definition of life is being used. The survey therefore rejects the claims that all biological molecules are literally right-handed, that one origin mechanism is known, or that current knowledge proves achiral life impossible.

## Idea result

The selected direction, `D2`, is **Stereochemical Closure, Not a Preferred Hand**. It hypothesizes that a chiral polymer-based chemical-life architecture needs a stable compatibility relation, not necessarily the particular L/D sign used by terrestrial biology. The primary test asks whether closure predicts registered capabilities more coherently than the broad phrase ``life requires chirality.'' Its falsification conditions include well-supported mixed or achiral alternatives, unstable evidence coding, and a conclusion that disappears when the life-definition contract is varied.

## ExperimentDesign result

The proposed protocol is a source-bound evidence matrix, not a wet-lab study. Records are coded as homochiral closure, cross-chiral closure, mixed unclosed, or achiral/unknown. Capability fields cover template continuity, reproducible structure, selective recognition, heritable information, and support for open-ended evolution. Two domain-qualified reviewers must code a pilot set with citations, source passages, ambiguity flags, and adjudication records. Permitted outputs are conditional support, alternative architecture support, insufficient evidence, and definition-sensitive abstention. Prohibited outputs include a claim to have created life, proved a universal law, or demonstrated a mirror organism.

## Conclusion

Known life needs stereochemistry because its molecular machines operate in a chiral geometrical world. Homochirality efficiently reduces ambiguity and makes compatible replication, folding, and recognition possible. But the best current conclusion is architectural rather than absolute: persistent stereochemical closure appears strongly important for known and many plausible chiral polymer systems; its universal status across all possible definitions and realizations of life is still unestablished. The research report therefore treats chirality as a high-value biosignature and functional constraint, not as a settled, definition-independent law of life.
