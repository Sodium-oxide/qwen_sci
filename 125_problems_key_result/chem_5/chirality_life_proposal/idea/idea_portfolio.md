## Selected primary idea: D2

**Title:** *Stereochemical Closure, Not a Preferred Hand: A Tiered Test of Chirality's Functional Necessity for Chemical Life*

**Central hypothesis:** For a stipulated chemical-life architecture that uses chiral informational or functional polymers, durable heredity and selective function require a **stereochemical closure**: a reproducible compatibility relation among monomers, templates, catalysts, and recognition partners. Homochirality is the most clearly evidenced terrestrial realization of this closure, but a cross-chiral or mirror-related closure can be conceptually distinct. A universal chirality claim is justified only if it survives explicit comparison against admissible alternative architectures under a stated definition of life.

**Mechanism:** Mixed stereochemical inputs can impair an architecture when they prevent polymer extension, disrupt repeatable folding, or corrupt recognition. Selection, crystallization, autocatalysis, or compartmentalized feedback may amplify a compatible convention. The convention's sign is not the target; the network-level capacity to retain and use stereochemical information is.

**Falsification conditions:**

1. Evidence synthesis finds no reproducible association between stated closure regimes and template, folding, or recognition capability after source-quality control.
2. A well-supported mixed or achiral architecture meets the pre-registered heredity and selective-function criteria without a stereochemical closure.
3. The proposed coding cannot distinguish ordinary homochirality from cross-chiral complementarity with acceptable reviewer agreement.
4. Conclusions change materially when the life-definition contract is varied and no conditional statement remains stable.

**MCTS-style evolution summary:** The root defect was an all-or-nothing question: ``Does life require chirality?'' The retained edits were: split chirality from homochirality; replace a preferred-handedness claim with compatibility relations; add cross-chiral counterexamples; and require a definition-sensitive decision rule. The discarded edit was extrapolating from terrestrial L/D conventions to all possible life.
