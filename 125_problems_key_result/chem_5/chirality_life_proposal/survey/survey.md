# Survey Agent: Chirality, Homochirality, and the Scope of Life

## Research question

Why does life require chirality, and does the homochirality of terrestrial biopolymers establish a universal requirement for life? The question contains three distinct claims that must not be conflated:

1. **Molecular chirality:** an individual molecule can exist as non-superimposable mirror forms.
2. **Biological homochirality:** a polymer family or biochemical network preferentially uses one compatible stereochemical convention.
3. **Universal necessity:** every chemically possible system that counts as life must have chirality or homochirality.

The first is a chemical definition, the second is a well-supported terrestrial regularity, and the third remains an open philosophical and scientific claim.

## Evidence synthesis

Known biology is chiral at several coupled scales. Proteins are overwhelmingly built from L-amino acids; nucleic-acid sugars have the D configuration. The point is not that all living molecules have one visual ``right hand.'' Rather, a common stereochemical convention lets a polymer fold reproducibly, lets a catalyst discriminate substrates, and lets molecular recognition preserve the geometry on which a network depends. A receptor, enzyme, or nucleic-acid template can therefore be described as a lock whose useful interaction depends on compatible stereochemistry.

Template-directed RNA chemistry gives a concrete mechanism. Joyce *et al.* reported that poly(C)-directed oligomerization proceeded readily with monomers of the same handedness as the template and much less efficiently with opposite-handed monomers; in a racemic mixture, opposite-handed incorporation could terminate extension [S2]. This is evidence for a functional bottleneck in a particular nonenzymatic RNA-like replication setting, not proof that every living system must use that setting. Chen and Ma's later computational model treats chiral selection and polymer-level symmetry breaking as a possible co-emergent feature of RNA-world evolution [S3]. The paper is a model result and must not be elevated to a historical observation of early Earth.

Experimental and theoretical work supplies mechanisms for producing or amplifying asymmetry, but not a settled origin story. Ozturk *et al.* reported enantioenrichment and subsequent homochiral crystallization of a racemic RNA precursor on magnetite under a proposed prebiotic setting [S5]. Blackmond, Hein and Blackmond, and Sallembien *et al.* review coupled chemical and physical routes to enantioenrichment [S1, S6, S8]. These lines of evidence support plausibility of multiple routes; they do not identify the unique route that established terrestrial biology.

The strongest reason not to call homochirality a proved universal law is that chirality can be organized differently. Sczepanski and Joyce developed a cross-chiral RNA polymerase ribozyme: a D-RNA enzyme operated on L-RNA components, with the enantiomeric relation also demonstrated [S4]. This does not create a mirror organism and does not settle whether an entirely achiral evolvable chemistry exists. It does show that the simple rule ``all interacting biological macromolecules must share one handedness'' is too strong. A mirror or cross-chiral architecture may preserve functional complementarity through a different stereochemical relation.

Finally, the scope of the word *life* matters. NASA's working definition, ``a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution,'' foregrounds chemistry, persistence, and evolvability but does not explicitly stipulate a particular terrestrial handedness [S9]. Definitions centered on metabolism, autopoiesis, information, or adaptive evolution may select different necessary conditions. The defensible conclusion is conditional: homochirality is strongly supported as an enabling architecture for terrestrial DNA/RNA/protein biochemistry and many candidate chiral replicator systems; its universality across all possible life is unproven.

## Evidence-quality notes

- [S2] and [S4] are primary experimental demonstrations of chirality-dependent or cross-chiral RNA function.
- [S5] is a primary prebiotic-chemistry demonstration; it supports one plausible enrichment route, not life's historical origin.
- [S3] is a computational RNA-world model and is marked model-dependent.
- [S1], [S6], and [S8] are synthesis/review sources used for framing, not as direct mechanistic proof.
- [S7] provides simulations comparing mixed- and homochiral protein libraries; it is not an experimental reconstruction of early life.
- [S9] is a definition source used to identify scope dependence, not experimental evidence about chirality.
