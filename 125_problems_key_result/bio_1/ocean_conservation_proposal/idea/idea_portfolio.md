# Idea Agent Portfolio: Climate-Resilient Ocean-Conservation Portfolios

## Evidence-constrained reframing

The Survey handoff rejects a universal ``best ocean solution.'' The design problem is to identify a connected portfolio that can reduce land-sea pressures while respecting the fact that nutrient management, plastic leakage, habitat condition, fishing pressure, governance capacity, and livelihood impacts have different mechanisms and uncertainties.

## Selected direction: CoastWeave

**Title:** *CoastWeave: An Equity-Constrained, Climate-Robust Portfolio Model for Land-Sea Coastal Conservation*

**Scientific object:** a specified coastal seascape represented by linked watershed, coastal-habitat, fisheries, waste-leakage, observing, governance, and livelihood modules.

**Central hypothesis:** a portfolio selected with explicit climate scenarios, implementation-capacity gates, and equity constraints will differ materially from a portfolio chosen only to maximize aggregate ecological benefit. In particular, it will avoid apparently efficient options that are unmonitorable, infeasible, or impose concentrated livelihood burdens.

**Portfolio levers:** nutrient-source reduction, plastic-leakage prevention, coastal habitat protection/restoration, spatial fisheries/area-based protection, monitoring/data access, and governance/capacity strengthening. The model selects types and combinations of levers; it does not prescribe operations, restrictions, locations, budgets, permits, or stakeholder behavior.

**Falsifiable predictions:**

1. At least one ecological-only portfolio will violate a declared equity, capacity, or observability gate.
2. At least one portfolio ranking will change when climate/runoff and socioeconomic scenarios are varied.
3. A robust, gated portfolio will preserve a higher minimum performance across scenarios than a nominally optimal single-scenario portfolio, or the climate-robust framing is not justified.

## Simulated portfolio evolution

| Step | Detected flaw | Route | Refinement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root idea | ``Ocean conservation'' has no testable unit | Scope decomposition | Specify a coastal seascape and an intervention portfolio |
| Candidate A | Ecological optimum treats interventions as interchangeable | Mechanism separation | Use distinct nutrient, plastic, habitat, fisheries, monitoring, and governance modules |
| Candidate B | A model can hide who pays costs | Equity audit | Add non-compensatory livelihood and participation safeguards |
| Candidate C | Historical averages overstate resilience | Climate stress test | Compare scenario-dependent and robust portfolios |
| Selected D3 | Scoring can manufacture certainty | Evidence provenance and abstention | Attach evidence cards, transfer bounds, and `EVIDENCE_OR_MODEL_ABSTAIN` |

## Selection rationale

D3 is selected because it uses the Survey's entire gap ledger without claiming to solve a local management problem from a desk model. D1, D2, and D4 remain modular diagnostics within CoastWeave. The primary contribution is a transparent choice architecture: ecological benefit is necessary but cannot compensate for invalid capacity, legitimacy, equity, or observability conditions.

## Downstream guardrails

- The proposal is `DESIGN_ONLY`; it does not install infrastructure, alter catch rules, declare protected areas, restore habitat, recruit participants, or make policy decisions.
- Inputs must preserve geographic and social transfer limits; a global literature result cannot become a local effect size by default.
- A negative or missing evidence state is reported as a gap or abstention, not repaired by assumed benefits.
- Any field implementation would need independent ecological, Indigenous/local-community, legal, governance, and equity review.
