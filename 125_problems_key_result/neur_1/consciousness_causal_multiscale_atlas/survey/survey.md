# Survey Agent Report

## Research question and scientific reframing

**User question.** Where does consciousness lie?

**Operational question.** Which distributed neural and, if necessary, molecular processes are causally required for conscious experience, and at what spatial, temporal, and computational scale can leading theories be distinguished?

The question cannot be answered responsibly by pointing to one anatomical dot. Consciousness is a family of related targets: phenomenal experience (what an experience is like), access to information for flexible reasoning and action, report of that experience, and self-awareness. These targets overlap but are not identical. A person may have experience without being able to report it, and self-awareness is a more specific capacity than sentience. The Survey Agent therefore treats ``where'' as a question about causal organization: the minimal network, recurrent dynamics, and temporal scale that support experience, rather than a request for a single coordinate.

The current evidence supports a strong but bounded conclusion. Conscious experience depends on brain-wide biological dynamics, yet the field does not agree whether its constitutive substrate is best identified with posterior recurrent processing, frontoparietal/global broadcasting, higher-order metacognitive representation, integrated causal structure, predictive control, or a combination. Measures that correlate with report can be contaminated by attention, working memory, decision, motor preparation, and social compliance. A theory-neutral answer must therefore triangulate report, no-report behavior, perturbational responses, effective connectivity, and state/content measures.

The prompt also raises the Penrose-Hameroff Orch-OR proposal, which places a quantum process in neuronal microtubules and connects it to conscious moments. This is a legitimate scientific hypothesis to map and stress-test, but it is not an established explanation. The survey keeps it in the comparison set only when it makes an independent, scale-linked prediction that can be tested against neural-network and decoherence alternatives.

## Evidence map

### Correlates are not yet mechanisms

Koch, Massimini, Boly, and Tononi distinguish the neural correlates of consciousness from a complete explanation of how conscious experience is generated [1]. A neural signal can accompany a conscious report while being downstream of the experience, upstream of the report, or part of a broader state that supports both. This distinction changes the study design. If the target is a mechanism, passive recording is not enough; one needs interventions, carefully matched states, and a way to quantify whether changing a candidate process changes experience rather than merely changing the report apparatus.

Seth and Bayne's 2022 review organizes four prominent theoretical families: higher-order theories, global workspace theories, re-entry and predictive-processing theories, and integrated information theory [2]. Their publisher page was checked directly for the title, authors, journal, publication date, volume, pages, and abstract. The abstract makes the key methodological point: current theories make different neurobiological commitments, but it remains unclear how cleanly they relate to one another or whether current experiments can distinguish them. This is why the present project is framed as an adversarial, theory-comparison design rather than an attempt to declare a winner from one biomarker.

### The main theory families

**Global neuronal workspace (GNW).** GNW proposes that conscious access involves information becoming globally available to multiple systems through widespread recurrent broadcasting. Mashour *et al.* review this proposal and its relationship to ignition, long-range connectivity, and conscious access [3]. Dehaene, Lau, and Kouider similarly describe consciousness in terms of global availability and distinguish conscious processing from local, unconscious computations [5]. A strong prediction for the present study is a late, long-range, report-independent broadcasting signature that differs between conscious and unconscious access when sensory input and motor response are matched.

**Integrated information theory (IIT).** IIT begins with the intrinsic causal organization of a system. Tononi *et al.* argue that consciousness corresponds to integrated information and differentiated causal structure, with an empirical program linking this formal idea to neural dynamics [4]. A practical study cannot casually substitute an arbitrary complexity statistic for formal integrated information. It can, however, ask whether perturbational complexity and causal integration track conscious state and whether the spatial distribution of the relevant causal structure agrees with theory-specific predictions.

**Recurrent processing and predictive accounts.** Recurrent processing theories emphasize feedback loops, especially in posterior and sensory systems, while predictive-processing approaches emphasize hierarchical inference, precision, and the interaction of generative models with embodied signals [2]. Their predictions can diverge from a strict global-broadcast account on the timing and sufficiency of local recurrent activity. This makes perturbing posterior recurrence while holding report demands constant scientifically informative.

**Higher-order theories.** Higher-order accounts place emphasis on a representation of a mental state by another suitable representation, often involving metacognitive systems. Their key challenge is to distinguish the higher-order representation of an experience from confidence, decision, and verbal report. A no-report design with confidence and metacognitive measures separated is therefore essential.

**Orch-OR.** Hameroff and Penrose propose that consciousness arises from orchestrated objective reductions associated with quantum processes in neuronal microtubules [10]. This proposal is more specific than the vague claim that ``the universe is conscious'': it locates the hypothesized mechanism at a molecular scale and proposes a coupling across microtubules, neurons, and experience. But the theory must meet the same standard as its competitors. A microtubule oscillation, a quantum-like mathematical description, or a high-frequency neural signal is not consciousness-specific. The proposal needs a reproducible molecular signature, a predicted timescale, a perturbation response, and a demonstrated link to conscious state that cannot be explained by ordinary network dynamics.

### No-report and perturbational evidence

Tsuchiya *et al.* explain why no-report paradigms are important: asking a participant to report changes the task and recruits processes that may be mistaken for neural correlates of experience [6]. No-report does not mean ``no behavior'' or ``no measurement.'' It means the conscious-state manipulation is assessed through an independently validated proxy, such as involuntary physiological or behavioral responses, while report-related motor and metacognitive demands are minimized. The design must still validate that the proxy tracks the intended experience and must analyze residual report conditions.

Casali *et al.* introduced the perturbational complexity index (PCI), combining a perturbation with a complexity measure to distinguish conscious levels across wakefulness, sleep, and anesthesia [7]. PCI-like measures are valuable state-level tools, especially when passive complexity is ambiguous. They do not by themselves locate the content-generating substrate or prove IIT. Boly *et al.* review the front-versus-back debate and illustrate why a topographic disagreement can persist when different studies target different definitions of correlate, content, state, and report [8].

The mature experimental strategy is therefore triangulation. Use content-matched conscious/unconscious conditions; record at multiple scales; include no-report and report conditions; perturb candidate nodes or loops; test model predictions out of sample; and distinguish state markers from content and mechanism markers. The field needs results that can reduce uncertainty between theories, not only measures that rise whenever a participant says ``yes.''

## Subhypotheses and evidence coverage

### SH-1: Conscious experience requires a distributed causal process, not a single isolated site

The claim does not entail that every brain region contributes equally. It predicts that the minimal causal substrate will include a network and a time window in which perturbing a critical process changes experience. Posterior sensory systems, frontoparietal broadcast, thalamocortical loops, and their interactions are competing candidate components. Passive anatomical localization is insufficient. **Coverage:** strong motivation, unresolved causal location.

### SH-2: Report-independent markers will retain a theory-discriminating component

If a signature disappears entirely when report is removed, it may be a report marker rather than an experience marker. The stronger prediction is not that all activity is report-independent, but that at least some theory-specific dynamics survive report removal and remain linked to a validated conscious-state manipulation. **Coverage:** no-report methods support the requirement; exact signatures remain unsettled.

### SH-3: Perturbational complexity tracks conscious state but does not alone settle content or theory

A perturbation can reveal causal capacity and complexity that passive recording misses. However, complexity is a measurement family, not a metaphysical proof. **Coverage:** PCI supports a state assay; the spatial and mechanistic interpretation remains open.

### SH-4: Competing theories make separable predictions in timing, topology, and intervention response

GNW predicts long-range ignition and access; IIT predicts integrated causal structure; recurrent accounts predict sufficiency of feedback; higher-order accounts predict a metacognitive representational contribution; predictive accounts predict precision-weighted hierarchical changes. **Coverage:** theory reviews support the comparison; preregistered quantitative predictions are still needed.

### SH-5: A microtubule-scale mechanism should leave an independent molecular/quantum signature coupled to experience

Orch-OR becomes scientifically differentiable only if its proposed microtubule process has a timing, perturbation, or coherence signature that tracks conscious state beyond synaptic/network models. Tegmark's decoherence analysis raises a timescale challenge [11], while Hagan, Hameroff, and Tuszynski argue that the conclusion depends on biological assumptions and model parameters [12]. The disagreement is exactly why a measurement-specific, scale-linked test is needed. **Coverage:** hypothesis and counterargument exist; no consensus marker has been established.

## Research gaps admitted to the ledger

The Survey Agent accepts six gaps. `GAP-01` is the unresolved transition from correlation to causal necessity. `GAP-02` is the report/metacognition confound. `GAP-03` is the lack of common preregistered theory-discriminating predictions. `GAP-04` is the unresolved spatial and temporal scale. `GAP-05` is the missing bridge between Orch-OR's molecular proposal and a consciousness-specific experience measure. `GAP-06` is the gap between state-level complexity markers and content/mechanism claims.

The survey rejects three shortcuts. Consciousness is not established as located in one confirmed point. A quantum vibration in microtubules is not proof that consciousness is quantum. And a verbal report is not identical to the subjective experience it reports. These rejections do not diminish the importance of the theories; they specify the tests required for a positive result.

## Survey conclusion and handoff

Consciousness most plausibly lies in a dynamically integrated biological process rather than in a single anatomical location, but ``plausibly'' is not a settled mechanism. The strongest next step is a **Causal Multiscale Consciousness Atlas (CMCA)**: a pre-registered, no-report-aware, perturbational study that compares theory-specific predictions across posterior recurrence, global broadcasting, higher-order/metacognitive dynamics, integrated complexity, and a separately measured molecular bridge. Its output is a causal evidence vector and a theory comparison, not a single consciousness coordinate.

The Idea Agent receives six hard constraints: separate experience, access, report, and self-awareness; use report-matched and no-report conditions; combine passive recordings with perturbations; compare theory-specific predictions out of sample; keep Orch-OR as a falsifiable competitor; and report no observed data because the downstream workflow is `DESIGN_ONLY`. The canonical structured boundary is stored in the accompanying evidence plan, gap ledger, and idea handoff.
