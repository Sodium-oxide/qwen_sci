# Idea Agent Report

## Intake and constraints

The Idea Agent received the Survey handoff with six accepted gaps. It must distinguish phenomenal experience from access, report, and self-awareness; avoid declaring one brain location from correlation; use no-report and perturbational tests; compare theories out of sample; treat Orch-OR as a falsifiable competitor; and remain `DESIGN_ONLY`.

## Reconstructing the problem

The surface question, ``Where does consciousness lie?'', assumes that consciousness is a thing with a single address. A more productive reconstruction is:

> Which distributed process is necessary for a specified conscious experience, at what spatial and temporal scale, and how can that process be distinguished from the machinery that reports, attends to, remembers, or evaluates the experience?

This reconstruction produces a hierarchy of claims. A **state marker** distinguishes wakefulness or anesthesia from unconsciousness. A **content marker** distinguishes seeing red from seeing green or seeing a stimulus from not seeing it. A **report marker** predicts a verbal, button, or confidence response. A **mechanism marker** changes when the candidate process is perturbed and changes validated experience proxies while surviving report controls. The original prompt primarily asks for the last category, while much of the literature naturally supplies the first three.

## Candidate directions

### Candidate A: passive localization with fMRI

This direction would compare conscious and unconscious trials and identify the largest activation cluster. It is easy to communicate but scientifically weak: vascular activity is slow, distributed, and correlational; the result is sensitive to task, report, and preprocessing. It is rejected as the primary idea, though imaging remains a useful component of a multiscale design.

### Candidate B: no-report posterior-hot-zone adjudication

This candidate uses involuntary or indirect measures to reduce report confounds and compares posterior recurrent activity with frontoparietal broadcasting. It directly addresses `GAP-02` and `GAP-04`. It remains incomplete because no-report proxies need validation, and a correlate still needs a perturbational causal test.

### Candidate C: perturbational complexity index

This candidate uses a controlled perturbation and response complexity to identify a causal capacity associated with conscious state. It is well motivated by PCI, but a state-level complexity index alone does not locate experience content, distinguish IIT from all other theories, or adjudicate Orch-OR.

### Candidate D: microtubule quantum bridge stress test

This candidate directly tests the Penrose-Hameroff proposal. It is important because the prompt explicitly presents it, but a molecular oscillation could be epiphenomenal, downstream, or a measurement artifact. It also risks asking an in-vitro molecular preparation to answer a question about whole-organism experience. It is retained as an independent adjudication module, not the sole atlas.

## Selected direction: Causal Multiscale Consciousness Atlas

The selected primary idea combines Candidates B and C, and includes D as a constrained competitor. The **Causal Multiscale Consciousness Atlas (CMCA)** does not search for a single bright spot. It generates an evidence vector across four dimensions:

| Atlas dimension | Question | Evidence needed | Failure meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| State | Is the system capable of conscious processing? | Perturbational complexity and state-sensitive dynamics | A state marker is absent or non-specific |
| Content | Which experience is present? | Content-matched decoding and validated no-report proxy | A state change does not identify content |
| Causality | Which process is necessary? | Targeted perturbation with report-matched and no-report outcomes | Correlation may be downstream or report-related |
| Theory | Which model predicts the joint pattern? | Preregistered out-of-sample model comparison | Flexible narratives remain underconstrained |

The molecular module adds a fifth, separate question: does an Orch-OR-specific molecular/quantum observable have an independent timing and perturbation relationship to the conscious-state manipulation that survives neural-network and decoherence nulls? If not, the molecular claim loses support even if neural consciousness measures remain positive.

## Theory-discriminating matrix

CMCA makes each theory commit to a measurable pattern before data are viewed:

- **GNW:** a late, long-range, report-independent broadcasting/ignition event should predict flexible access across systems.
- **IIT:** integrated differentiation and causal complexity should track conscious state and be sensitive to perturbational structure, not simply signal amplitude.
- **Recurrent processing:** posterior/sensory feedback should be an earlier sufficient process for conscious content than later report-related broadcast.
- **Higher-order/predictive accounts:** higher-order or precision-weighted hierarchical dynamics should add explanatory power beyond sensory recurrence, while not being reducible to confidence or motor report.
- **Orch-OR:** a molecular/quantum process associated with microtubules should make a specific, independently measurable, time-locked prediction coupled to the experience manipulation.

The first four are families rather than single equations. The ExperimentDesign Agent must write the final parametric restrictions and priors with domain experts, then freeze them. The Idea Agent rejects an analysis in which a theory is allowed to explain every possible timing, region, or measure after results are known.

## Mechanism and falsifiability

The central hypothesis is that conscious experience depends on a dynamically integrated causal process with a nontrivial spatial and temporal extent. This hypothesis is falsifiable because it requires: (i) an experience-sensitive effect under a no-report or report-matched condition; (ii) an intervention that changes the effect rather than only the motor/report channel; (iii) a reproducible pattern across sessions and participants; and (iv) out-of-sample theory prediction. A candidate that appears only during button pressing is a report marker. A candidate that distinguishes wakefulness but not conscious content is a state marker. A candidate that changes with a perturbation but is also changed by an unconscious control is not yet a mechanism marker.

The Orch-OR claim is tested by a sharper criterion. It is not enough to detect microtubule vibration or quantum-like noise. A positive result must show that a prespecified molecular signature changes with a conscious-state manipulation, has the predicted timing/scale, is independently measured, and is not explained by ordinary synaptic/network dynamics, thermal noise, or measurement coupling. A null result would not prove that all quantum effects are absent from biology; it would reject the specified consciousness-generating bridge under the tested conditions.

## Selection rationale and handoff

CMCA directly addresses all six Survey gaps. It turns correlation into intervention (`GAP-01`), separates report from experience (`GAP-02`), requires pre-registered predictions (`GAP-03`), resolves spatial and temporal scale as an empirical output (`GAP-04`), gives Orch-OR an explicit adjudication criterion (`GAP-05`), and separates state-level complexity from content/mechanism claims (`GAP-06`). It is ambitious: if the evidence converges, it could locate a causal network and sharply reduce the theory space. It is also disciplined: it does not treat an attractive biomarker or a molecular oscillation as a solution.

The ExperimentDesign Agent is instructed to build D0--D5: preregistration and construct validation; report/no-report conscious-content conditions; multiscale recording; targeted perturbation; theory model comparison; and an independent molecular bridge stress test. The Author Agent must retain the conditional nature of the conclusion and must not invent human results, organoid consciousness, or quantum effects.
