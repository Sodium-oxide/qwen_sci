# Experiment Design - Testing BRIDGE Without Overstating Cross-Species Mechanism

## Aim and comparison

The study tests whether observable human attachment to dogs and cats is best explained by partner-specific biological regulation rather than nonspecific exposure. It does not test whether animals have human-like subjective experience.

Recruit 180 established dyads: 90 dog-owner and 90 cat-owner pairs. Enroll a matched familiar-nonowner for each animal whenever possible. A longitudinal adoption substudy follows 40 newly formed dyads from the first week through month six, but its results are kept separate from the established-dyad confirmatory analysis.

## Experimental session

Each dyad completes four 12-minute, counterbalanced conditions on separate days at the same circadian window:

1. owner free interaction;
2. familiar-nonowner free interaction;
3. owner present behind a transparent barrier with no touch;
4. low-arousal object/quiet-rest control.

Food, toys, task order, movement space, temperature, and sample-collection schedule are fixed or recorded. Animals can disengage at any time; no restraint, deprivation, or intranasal hormone administration occurs.

## Measurements

| Domain | Human | Dog/cat | Primary use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Behavior | gaze direction, touch, posture, speech/acoustic features, movement | voluntary approach, gaze/head orientation, contact, vocalization, withdrawal | partner-specific affiliation and cue weighting |
| Autonomic | ECG-derived heart-rate variability, electrodermal activity | collar ECG or validated pulse/HRV device, movement | time-resolved arousal and synchrony |
| Endocrine | saliva cortisol and validated peripheral oxytocin protocol | species-validated saliva/urine cortisol and oxytocin protocol where feasible | conditional peripheral association only |
| Development/health | sleep, medication, reproductive status, acute illness, caregiving duration | age, sex, neuter status, breed/ancestry, early socialization, veterinary health | confounding and moderation |
| Genetics | optional saliva SNP panel with ancestry safeguards | optional species-appropriate SNP panel | exploratory moderation with replication barrier |

## Primary endpoint

The primary behavioral endpoint is a preregistered, species-specific affiliation composite. Its ingredients are standardized within species and locked before enrollment. A positive primary result requires an owner-versus-familiar-nonowner contrast and no contradiction from the object/quiet control. It is not enough that a dyad looks affectionate.

## Analysis

Use a hierarchical mixed model with dyad random intercepts, condition, species, cue class, session order, and health/development covariates. Analyze endocrine values using matrix-specific quality controls and report collection failures. Test co-regulation only with time-lagged models compared against movement-, schedule-, and room-shared null models. Use planned missing-data rules and a held-out prediction set.

## Decision rules

- Stop a molecular/genetic interpretation if sample integrity, assay precision, or ancestry/breed control fails.
- Downgrade a dyadic-regulation claim if the time-lagged model does not beat the shared-exposure model.
- Report dog and cat estimates separately before any pooled estimate.
- Treat any OXTR association as exploratory until independently replicated in a preregistered cohort.
- Publish de-identified behavioral code, assay SOPs, and model specifications; do not release re-identifiable human genomic data or location data.

## Welfare and inference safeguards

Veterinary and human-research oversight is required. Animals are enrolled only when healthy enough for noninvasive observation, and an observer stops a session for sustained avoidance or physiological distress. Sampling personnel, behavioral coders, and analysts receive blinded identifiers. The final report must separate direct observations, statistical associations, mechanistic candidates, and unsupported interpretations.
