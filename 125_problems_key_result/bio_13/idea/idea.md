# Idea - BRIDGE: Biological Routes to Interspecies Dyadic Guided Engagement

## Reframed question

For a specified human-companion-animal dyad, which evolutionary, sensory, physiological, developmental, and genetic variables make partner-specific affiliation more likely than a response to novelty, food, handling, or general sociability?

## Framework

**BRIDGE** prevents a broad statement such as "people love pets" from being mistaken for a single mechanism.

| Stage | Question | Required evidence | Claim allowed |
|---|---|---|---|
| B - Background selection | Did species history create a human-compatible ecological niche? | Domestication, comparative, and genomic evidence | Enabling evolutionary context |
| R - Recognition | Does the animal and human discriminate the familiar partner through gaze, odor, voice, touch, or movement? | Counterbalanced familiar/nonowner and sensory-control trials | Partner-specific cue response |
| I - Integrated state | Do autonomic, HPA-axis, or peptide measures change with the interaction? | Validated assays, time course, pre-specified matrices | Peripheral physiological association |
| D - Dyad regulation | Does one partner's measured state predict a time-lagged change in the other beyond shared environment? | Synchronized time series and shared-exposure controls | Candidate co-regulation |
| G - Genotype and development | Do genotype, age, early socialization, and health modify the response? | Stratified or longitudinal design, ancestry/breed controls | Moderation, not direct causation |
| E - Evidence calibration | Which lower-level alternatives remain? | Replication, model comparison, held-out prediction | Bounded mechanistic conclusion |

## Falsifiable hypotheses

1. **H1 - Partner specificity.** Owner interaction will increase a preregistered behavioral affiliation index relative to a matched familiar-nonowner interaction in both species after accounting for food expectation and activity.
2. **H2 - Species-differentiated cues.** The owner effect will be more strongly predicted by mutual gaze in dogs, whereas cat approach initiation, voluntary proximity, and tactile interaction will explain more variance in cats. This is a directional prediction, not a claim that cats do not use visual cues.
3. **H3 - Conditional physiology.** The size and direction of peripheral oxytocin and cortisol changes will depend on species, assay matrix, interaction quality, and baseline state. A null cat group mean remains compatible with behavior-specific associations.
4. **H4 - Dyadic timing.** After controlling for shared movement and room context, time-lagged owner-animal autonomic coupling will be stronger in owner than nonowner trials. Failure to outperform the shared-exposure model falsifies a co-regulation claim.
5. **H5 - Developmental moderation.** Early social exposure and temperament will moderate partner-specific responses; exploratory OXTR associations will not be called causal without independent replication.

## Best explanatory model

Attachment is proposed to emerge when four conditions coincide: (i) a species has a domestication-compatible route into human habitats, (ii) repeated interaction makes an individual human a reliable multisensory predictor of safety and resources, (iii) conserved mammalian reward and stress-regulation systems reinforce voluntary contact, and (iv) developmental and genetic variation tunes the magnitude of that response. Dogs and cats may reach this endpoint through partially overlapping but nonidentical pathways. BRIDGE therefore predicts convergence in observable affiliation with divergence in dominant sensory and endocrine signatures.

## Claim ladder

- **Level 1:** An owner and an animal behave differently from a matched nonowner condition.
- **Level 2:** The difference remains after controls for food, handler cues, activity, novelty, and schedule.
- **Level 3:** A pre-specified physiological measure covaries with behavior under validated collection and assay procedures.
- **Level 4:** A time-lagged, controlled dyadic model outperforms shared-environment explanations.
- **Level 5:** A causal neural, molecular, or genetic claim requires a targeted perturbation or independently replicated longitudinal evidence. This project is not designed to make Level 5 claims.

## Design-only boundary

BRIDGE is a proposal and evidence-synthesis framework. It reports no newly collected hormone values, genetic effects, behavioral effect sizes, or human/animal health outcomes.
