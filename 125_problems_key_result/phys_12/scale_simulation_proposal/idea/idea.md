# Idea Agent Output: Scale-Resolved Simulation Credibility Ledger (SRCL)

## Candidate search and selection

The Survey handoff rejects both “macroscale is automatically easy” and “quantum computers are necessary for every microscale problem.” The Idea Agent generated four constrained directions:

1. **Universal quantum-supremacy score.** Rejected: it has no quantity-of-interest, classical baseline, or error-budget contract.
2. **Macro-only numerical convergence checklist.** Rejected as too narrow: it omits atomistic, quantum, mapping, and hardware constraints.
3. **Scale-Resolved Simulation Credibility Ledger (SRCL).** Selected: it uses one quantity-of-interest ledger across macro PDE, atomistic/classical micro, classical quantum, NISQ, and fault-tolerant projection cards.
4. **Hardware-only quantum benchmark dashboard.** Retained as a competitive component but insufficient because device statistics are not physical-model validation.

## Selected primary idea

**Title:** Scale-Resolved Simulation Credibility Ledger for Error-Bounded Classical--Quantum Comparison

**Target gaps:** `GAP-ACCURACY-001`, `GAP-CROSSSCALE-002`.

**Central hypothesis.** A simulation claim becomes comparable across scales only when it declares a quantity of interest, tolerance, model domain, error decomposition, reference/validation route, and resource budget. Under that ledger, many tasks will remain classically preferable, while a quantum pathway becomes a candidate only when its mapping, algorithmic, sampling, and hardware errors can plausibly close the same tolerance budget.

**Mechanism.** SRCL decomposes the planned total error into model discrepancy, parameter/input uncertainty, discretization or representation error, solver/algorithm error, sampling/measurement error, coupling error, and, for quantum cards, mapping, compilation, noise, mitigation/correction, and logical-resource terms. It records error dependence rather than assuming all terms are independent. A candidate receives a status only after a comparable classical baseline and an evidence route are attached.

**Falsifiers.** SRCL fails if (a) a pathway is ranked accurate without a quantity of interest and tolerance; (b) a quantum candidate is claimed superior without a classical baseline or hardware/mapping ledger; (c) a macro result passes numerical verification but is presented as physically validated without validation evidence; or (d) dominance labels change across declared sensitivity ranges without reporting the switch.

| Portfolio class | Direction | Decision |
