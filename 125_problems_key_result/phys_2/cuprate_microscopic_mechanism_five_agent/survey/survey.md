# Survey: Microscopic Mechanism of Cuprate High-Temperature Superconductivity

## Scope and question

This survey asks a deliberately narrower question than ``why is every high-Tc
superconductor superconducting?'' The target is the copper-oxide family, where
phase-sensitive experiments establish a predominantly d-wave order parameter,
but the interaction that produces pairing and its relationship to the Mott,
pseudogap, charge-order, and strange-metal regimes remain unresolved. The
working requirement for a useful mechanism is not a post-hoc narrative: it must
connect a material's normal-state degrees of freedom to a sign-changing pairing
kernel and predict a measurement capable of failing.

## Verified evidence anchors

1. Tsuei and Kirtley review phase-sensitive experiments and report that the
   pairing-symmetry controversy is, for a number of optimally doped hole- and
   electron-doped cuprates, settled in favor of predominantly d-wave symmetry.
   This constrains any candidate interaction to favor a sign-changing gap.
2. Keimer *et al.* describe the cuprate phase diagram as unusually complex and
   emphasize unresolved collective fluctuations and the normal state. Their
   review supports the gap statement that one isolated order parameter is not
   yet an adequate mechanism-level account.
3. Scalapino reviews Hubbard-like descriptions and proposes spin-fluctuation
   mediated pairing as a common thread among unconventional superconductors.
   This is a leading, physically motivated hypothesis, not a settled proof for
   every cuprate or every region of its phase diagram.
4. Comin and Damascelli review resonant x-ray evidence for charge order in
   cuprates. Charge order is therefore a measured broken-symmetry phenomenon
   that a mechanism model must accommodate, either as a competitor, a partner,
   or a secondary consequence.
5. LeBlanc *et al.* benchmark the square-lattice Hubbard model with many
   numerical algorithms and emphasize thermodynamic-limit extrapolation and
   method-dependent uncertainties. This establishes a computational gap: a
   model can be physically motivated yet insufficiently controlled to settle
   the microscopic question by itself.

## Evidence-bound synthesis

The d-wave constraint and the strong correlation phenomenology motivate a
spin-mediated pairing channel whose momentum structure changes sign between
antinodal sectors. However, spin strength alone does not explain why the
superconducting dome is asymmetric, why an underdoped pseudogap removes low
energy spectral weight, or how charge order changes the phase space available
for pairing. Conversely, charge order alone does not supply a demonstrated
attractive d-wave interaction. A discriminating program should therefore
measure the magnetic channel and the coherent electronic phase space jointly
rather than ask either one to explain all observations in isolation.

## Correcting the prompt's record claim

The 2020 Nature paper reporting a carbonaceous sulfur hydride transition of
287.7 +/- 1.2 K at extreme pressure was retracted on 26 September 2022. It is
recorded as an excluded historical claim, not as support for the cuprate
mechanism, room-pressure superconductivity, or the quantitative model below.

## Accepted research gap

**GAP-HTSC-01.** There is no compact, falsifiable, cross-modal test that asks
whether a d-wave pairing scale in a cuprate can be predicted more faithfully by
magnetic spectral support alone or by magnetic support multiplied by the
momentum-selective coherent spectral weight left after pseudogap/charge-order
physics. The gap is operational: the competing models make different ordering
predictions for an underdoped trajectory while holding the magnetic channel
nonzero.
