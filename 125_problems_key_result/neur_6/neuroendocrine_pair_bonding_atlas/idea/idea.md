# Idea Agent Output: Neuroendocrine Pair-Bonding Causal Atlas

## Primary direction

**Neuroendocrine Pair-Bonding Causal Atlas (NPB-CA)** proposes a cross-species, multiscale atlas of how endocrine state, receptor-localized neuropeptide signaling, and mesocorticolimbic circuits move an organism from sexual motivation to partner-directed reward and then to durable pair-bond maintenance.

The innovation is a causal state-space framework rather than a search for a single “love chemical” or “love center.” It combines matched partner-cue assays, longitudinal neural measurements, endocrine sampling, receptor-specific perturbations in prairie voles, and harmonized human neuroimaging. The atlas uses the same measurable axes in both systems: partner-cue neural discrimination, NAc/VTA reward-circuit engagement, social approach specificity, stress-axis state, and persistence of partner preference after delay.

## Root mechanism

The candidate mechanism has four linked modules:

1. **Motivational gate:** hypothalamic and gonadal-axis state changes the gain on sexual motivation and social cue processing.
2. **Partner recognition:** oxytocin/vasopressin receptor fields in hypothalamic, septal, amygdalar, and pallidal circuits alter the selectivity of partner cues.
3. **Reward assignment:** VTA-to-NAc dopamine assigns incentive value during bond formation; D1/D2 receptor balance may change during maintenance.
4. **Stabilization and buffering:** repeated partner interaction engages plasticity and stress-buffering pathways, creating persistence without assuming that every social contact is a bond.

## MCTS-style route comparison

**Route A — Single-hormone explanation.** Low novelty, weak causal adequacy; rejected because it cannot explain receptor localization, temporal phases, or nonspecific effects.

**Route B — Human imaging-only atlas.** Strong translational appeal but limited causal resolution; retained as a component, not a complete direction.

**Route C — Prairie-vole receptor perturbation.** Strong causal leverage but limited direct human mapping; retained as the mechanistic anchor.

**Route D — NPB-CA.** Integrates Routes B and C with endocrine time series and shared outcome definitions. It dominates on gap coverage, falsifiability, and feasibility under a staged design. It is selected as `primary_direction`.

## Falsifiable predictions

- If neuropeptide-gated partner recognition is causal, receptor perturbation should reduce partner-cue discrimination more than locomotion or general social approach, after matching exposure and stress state.
- If dopamine participates differently in formation and maintenance, D1/D2 perturbations should produce phase-specific effects rather than a single monotonic decrease in all social behavior.
- If endocrine state gates the transition, sex-steroid and glucocorticoid measures should explain between-session gain changes after accounting for circuit and genotype variables.
- If the cross-species bridge is valid, a prespecified partner-versus-control neural contrast should share representational geometry across vole neural recordings and human imaging, while absolute anatomy and receptor expression remain species-specific.
- If there is no stable shared state, latent-state models should fail out-of-sample replication across cohorts or species; that failure is informative and would reject the strongest version of the atlas.

## Portfolio decision

The primary direction is ambitious but experimentally bounded. It does not claim to reproduce human subjective experience in an animal. It asks whether measurable circuit computations—cue discrimination, reward assignment, and persistence—are implemented by conserved neuroendocrine motifs. The study is a design proposal and contains no observed results.
