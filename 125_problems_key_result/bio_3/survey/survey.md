# Survey - Why Can Only Some Cells Become Other Cells?

## Scientific restatement

The premise is too absolute. Many differentiated cells do not normally produce unrelated cell types in their native tissue, but induced pluripotency and direct lineage reprogramming show that cellular identity can sometimes be changed under artificial conditions. The testable question is therefore: **which intrinsic state variables and niche signals determine whether a defined cell population can retain, lose, or regain competence to reach a specified target identity, and what safety constraints limit that transition?**

Cell potency is graded rather than binary. Pluripotent embryonic stem cells and induced pluripotent stem cells can generate derivatives of the adult body under appropriate differentiation conditions. Tissue-resident adult stem cells usually have narrower, lineage-associated outputs and participate in local maintenance and repair. Progenitors can divide and differentiate over a limited range. Mature cells typically occupy stable transcriptional and epigenetic states that make spontaneous identity changes unlikely. The relevant output must be defined experimentally: a marker-positive cell, a target transcriptional state, functional behavior, stable lineage identity, or safe tissue contribution are progressively stronger claims.

## Mechanistic evidence map

Cell fate is maintained by coupled gene-regulatory networks, chromatin accessibility, DNA methylation, histone modifications, transcription-factor occupancy, nuclear organization, signaling, metabolism, mechanics, and cell-cycle history. A stem-cell niche provides contact-dependent signals, secreted factors, extracellular matrix, physical constraints, immune context, and nutrient/oxygen conditions. These inputs change the probability of division, quiescence, self-renewal, differentiation, death, or migration. A transcriptomic snapshot alone cannot identify which input caused a fate decision.

Yamanaka-factor reprogramming established that selected transcription factors can push differentiated cells toward an induced pluripotent state. Direct lineage reprogramming further shows that some source-to-target transitions can occur without a stable pluripotent intermediate. These findings refute a universal irreversibility claim but do not demonstrate that every cell can become every other cell. Reprogramming efficiency, trajectory, genomic integrity, residual source identity, epigenetic memory, tissue function, integration, immune interaction, and tumor risk remain context dependent.

## Evidence and safety boundary

Evidence of an early target marker is not sufficient to establish complete reprogramming. A credible claim needs at least: source-cell identity, clonal or lineage provenance, a time-resolved target-state comparison, negative controls, functional assay relevant to the target, stability after withdrawal of inducing cues, and genomic/epigenetic safety evidence appropriate to the model. For therapeutic translation, cell-product identity, purity, residual undifferentiated cells, off-target lineages, genomic alterations, delivery, tumorigenicity, engraftment, and long-term host effects are independent constraints.

This project is DESIGN_ONLY. It does not perform reprogramming, culture a cell line, generate a cell therapy, measure conversion efficiency, claim functional rescue, or make a clinical recommendation.
