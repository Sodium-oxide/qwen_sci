# Idea: ARCHAIC

## Central idea

**ARCHAIC** means **Ancient-genome Reconstruction of Cross-Hominin Admixture and Introgression Claims**. It is a staged evidence framework for claims about reticulate ancestry among Homo sapiens, Neanderthals, Denisovans, and potentially unsampled archaic lineages. The central rule is that a claim about interbreeding is promoted only when the relevant chain is measured:

1. **A - Authenticity and ascertainment:** establish endogenous ancient-DNA signal, damage, contamination bounds, sample age, coverage, and comparability.
2. **R - Reference and representation:** declare genomes, population labels, outgroups, callability masks, reference build, and recombination representation.
3. **C - Competing demographic models:** compare no-gene-flow, ancestral-structure, incomplete-lineage-sorting, single-pulse, multi-pulse, and ghost-source explanations.
4. **H - Haplotype and tract reconstruction:** map local ancestry with uncertainty rather than treating every allele-sharing excess as a tract.
5. **A - Admixture direction and timing:** estimate direction, timing, pulse number, and mixture proportion only with identifiable contrasts and sensitivity bounds.
6. **I - Independent validation:** require another statistic, data partition, laboratory/library, or independent sample set to reproduce the inference label.
7. **C - Claim calibration:** report a direct-hybrid, gene-flow-supported, timing-supported, source-qualified, or functional claim without skipping levels.

## Research objective

Develop a preregistered analysis protocol that quantifies which cross-hominin gene-flow models best predict ancient and present-day genomic patterns, while separating direct evidence of hybrid ancestry from population-level admixture and from claims about adaptation.

## Hypotheses

- **H0 - tree/structure hypothesis:** genome-wide allele sharing and local genealogies can be explained by incomplete lineage sorting and ancestral population structure without a post-split gene-flow edge.
- **H1 - Neanderthal-to-modern-human hypothesis:** a model with gene flow from a Neanderthal-related source into ancestors of selected modern-human populations predicts allele-sharing and tract patterns better than H0.
- **H2 - Denisovan-to-modern-human hypothesis:** a model with one or more Denisovan-related sources improves prediction for specified populations over H0 and H1.
- **H3 - cross-archaic hybrid hypothesis:** a directly dated ancient genome contains chromosome-scale ancestry compatible with recent Neanderthal--Denisovan parentage rather than deep shared ancestry alone.
- **H4 - reciprocal/ghost-source hypothesis:** models allowing ancient-human-related input into Neanderthals or input from an unsampled archaic lineage fit specified genomic intervals better than one-direction models, subject to strong source-identity uncertainty.
- **H5 - adaptive-retention hypothesis:** a candidate introgressed haplotype has evidence for frequency or functional change beyond demography and linked selection; introgressed origin alone is insufficient.

## Innovation

ARCHAIC treats an admixture graph as a testable model, not as a historical illustration. It joins ancient-DNA authenticity, f-statistics, local genealogies, tract chronology, and held-out prediction in one auditable workflow. It also makes a key distinction: *a directly observed hybrid*, *gene flow detected in a population*, *a dated source-qualified pulse*, and *adaptive or functional retention* are different results with different thresholds.

## Design-only boundary

This proposal contains no newly sequenced fossils, present-day genomes, D statistics, f4 ratios, admixture fractions, tract lengths, dates, or functional assays. Every numerical threshold and model comparison is an analysis specification for a future approved study.
