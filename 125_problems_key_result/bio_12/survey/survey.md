# Survey: Did Ancient Humans Interbreed with Other Hominins?

## Scientific reframing

The useful scientific question is not whether fossils can be placed into fixed human-like categories. It is: **for a declared set of hominin lineages, genomic samples, time windows, and demographic alternatives, what evidence supports gene flow, its direction, timing, and retained genomic consequences?** In this survey, *interbreeding* denotes mating that produced descendants carrying ancestry across a lineage boundary; a genomic signal can establish retained gene flow without revealing the frequency, social setting, or phenotype of every historical mating.

## Established evidence

1. The draft Neandertal genome shared more derived variants with present-day Eurasians than with sub-Saharan African comparison samples, supporting gene flow into ancestors of non-African populations rather than a simple bifurcating tree (Green *et al.*, 2010, DOI: 10.1126/science.1188021).
2. The Denisova Cave genome defined a sister group to Neandertals and indicated a Denisovan contribution to present-day Melanesian genomes (Reich *et al.*, 2010, DOI: 10.1038/nature09710). This is not evidence for a uniform Denisovan contribution to every modern population.
3. The Denisova 11 genome was consistent with a Neanderthal mother and Denisovan father, providing direct evidence of a first-generation Neanderthal--Denisovan offspring (Slon *et al.*, 2018, DOI: 10.1038/s41586-018-0455-x).
4. Ancient modern-human genomes can document close Neanderthal ancestry on a genealogical time scale: an early modern human from Romania had a recent Neanderthal ancestor (Fu *et al.*, 2015, DOI: 10.1038/nature14558), and Initial Upper Paleolithic individuals from Bacho Kiro carried recent Neanderthal ancestry (Iasi *et al.*, 2021, DOI: 10.1038/s41586-021-03335-3).
5. Gene flow was not necessarily one directional. Genomic methods and ancient samples have also supported older movement from early modern-human-related populations into eastern Neanderthals (Kuhlwilm *et al.*, 2016, DOI: 10.1038/nature16544), while some deeply diverged source models remain conditional on available reference genomes.

## What the evidence does and does not identify

Genome-wide asymmetry in allele sharing, local ancestry tracts, and a directly sequenced hybrid are mutually reinforcing, but they answer different questions. A D statistic establishes an allele-sharing asymmetry under a specified quartet; it does not by itself identify the exact donor, calendar date, number of pulses, or social circumstances. A local ancestry tract can support an introgressed origin, but recombination, reference bias, and an unsampled source affect tract assignment and dates. A first-generation hybrid proves one cross-lineage reproductive event but cannot estimate population-wide mixture rates.

Incomplete lineage sorting, ancestral population structure, sample contamination, reference bias, recombination-map error, time-varying population size, and unsequenced hominin populations can imitate or distort parts of a simple admixture signal. Therefore an empirical study must compare explicit demographic alternatives rather than treating an archaic-like segment as self-interpreting.

## Evidence and method map

| Evidence layer | Primary question | Useful methods | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ancient DNA authenticity | Is the sequence endogenous and temporally credible? | terminal-damage profiles, contamination estimates, independent libraries | low coverage and preservation can restrict inference |
| Genome-wide allele sharing | Is a strict tree inadequate? | D/ABBA-BABA, f4, f4-ratio, block jackknife | cannot alone resolve pulse count or donor identity |
| Local ancestry | Where are likely introgressed segments? | HMMs, conditional random fields, ancestral recombination graphs | sensitive to source panel, recombination, and ILS |
| Temporal information | When was gene flow likely? | tract-length distributions, dated ancient genomes, model fitting | dates are model- and recombination-dependent |
| Direct ancestry | Was a cross-lineage ancestor recent for an individual? | chromosome-scale ancestry blocks, kinship inference | not a population frequency estimate |
| Functional consequence | Were retained alleles selected or functional? | allele-frequency, expression, phenotype, and selection models | introgression alone is not adaptive introgression |

## Literature boundary

The report will cite primary ancient-genome studies, formal admixture-statistics work, and demographic/ARG methods. It will not infer that all archaeological or morphological categories are genetically homogeneous, nor will it infer behavioral motives from DNA. It is a `DESIGN_ONLY` synthesis: it reports no new sequences, contamination estimates, tract maps, mixture fractions, dates, or functional effects.
