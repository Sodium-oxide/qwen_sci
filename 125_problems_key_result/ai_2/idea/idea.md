# Idea: A Conditional Intelligence Frontier

## Direction

`CONSTRAINED-GENERAL-INTELLIGENCE-PLASTICITY-BENCHMARK`

## Core question

Does human cognitive performance approach a reproducible upper boundary once high-range measurement, age, domain, practice, health, and environment are modeled explicitly, and can structured education or training shift that boundary or only improve task-specific performance?

## Central idea

Replace the phrase "limit to human intelligence" with a conditional intelligence frontier. For each person, task domain, age range, and environment, estimate a latent ability trajectory and its asymptote. The frontier is not a single IQ value. It is a vector of limits for fluid reasoning, crystallized knowledge, working memory, processing speed, spatial reasoning, and new-task learning. A general factor is estimated only as a higher-order summary of these domains.

The study combines four separations that are usually blurred:

1. **Score versus construct:** a change in a normed score is not automatically a change in latent `g`.
2. **Plateau versus test ceiling:** a flat score can arise from insufficient high-difficulty items, time limits, retest effects, or a true performance asymptote.
3. **Practice versus transfer:** improvement on trained items is not the same as improvement on untrained tasks.
4. **Constraint versus immutability:** a biological correlate of high performance can constrain a trajectory without fixing an unchangeable destiny.

## Hypotheses

### H0: Measurement-equivalence hypothesis

After high-range item calibration and practice adjustment, apparent plateaus will be reduced or relocate to particular domains. A global IQ plateau is not expected to be stable unless measurement invariance holds across age and cohort.

### H1: Environmental-shift hypothesis

Education, sleep, health, and cognitive enrichment will shift latent performance, with the largest effects in domains that depend on acquired strategies or knowledge. The shift will not be identical across domains or individuals.

### H2: Conditional-asymptote hypothesis

Within a fixed task family and resource condition, repeated learning curves will approach individual-specific asymptotes. These asymptotes will vary with age, baseline ability, domain, and health rather than forming one human-wide constant.

### H3: Transfer-limitation hypothesis

Adaptive training will produce larger gains on near-transfer tasks than on far-transfer tasks. Evidence for a limit to general intelligence requires a plateau in latent cross-domain performance, not only a plateau in the trained task.

### H4: Network-mediation hypothesis

Brain measures associated with processing efficiency and flexible network recruitment will partially mediate individual differences in the frontier. They will not fully determine the frontier, and compensatory network changes may preserve performance in some age or health strata.

## Why this is a useful idea

The idea yields positive and negative scientific outcomes. A stable latent asymptote after calibration would support a conditional limit for the tested ability. A training shift would show that the frontier is movable. Domain-specific asymptotes would reject the claim that a single number describes all intelligence. If neural measures predict the asymptote but not the intervention response, they are constraints or correlates rather than a complete mechanism. If high-range item calibration removes a plateau, the previous limit was psychometric.

## Study architecture

The proposed test is a multi-site, multi-age longitudinal study with a randomized training module and a neurocognitive sub-study. A balanced baseline sample covers adolescence, early adulthood, midlife, and later adulthood, with deliberate representation across education, language, socioeconomic conditions, and health status. Participants complete an item-response-calibrated cognitive battery using alternate forms. A randomized subset receives adaptive reasoning and working-memory training, while an active-control group receives equally engaging non-adaptive tasks. Untrained transfer tasks and delayed follow-up determine whether gains generalize.

EEG and structural/resting-state MRI are used in a consented sub-sample to test mediation and compensation hypotheses, not to diagnose intelligence from a scan. Environmental variables are measured longitudinally. The core analysis is a hierarchical latent-state model with age, practice, intervention, batch/site, and domain effects.

## Design boundary

This idea cannot establish an absolute maximum for every possible human, task, culture, or future tool. It can establish whether a conditional frontier is supported for declared tasks and conditions. It is design-only: no participant data, effect size, causal result, or biological limit is claimed in the present report.
