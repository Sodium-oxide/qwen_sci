# Idea Agent: CURA-Turb

## Candidate directions

1. **Single universal exponent catalogue.** Collect fitted structure-function exponents across data sets. Rejected: exponent agreement alone cannot distinguish finite-window artifacts, intermittency, anisotropy, or different cascade directions.
2. **Exact-law-only benchmark.** Center every comparison on the four-fifths law. Retained as a component control but rejected as the full answer because it has a narrow assumption set and does not classify higher-order or non-HIT statistics.
3. **CURA-Turb: Conditional Universality and Regime Audit for Turbulence.** A source-bounded, multi-regime protocol that classifies an invariance claim before testing it and prevents statistics from moving between flow classes without an explicit transfer bridge.

## Selected primary idea

**CURA-Turb** uses an invariance dossier for each proposed statistic:

`claim type x statistic x regime card x scale interval x estimator x uncertainty/evidence card`.

It first asks whether a quantity is an exact equation symmetry, an exact conditional relation, a conditional similarity claim, or an empirical cross-flow regularity. It then compares only compatible regime cards and reports whether the evidence supports an exact-law residual check, conditional universality, intermittency-modified scaling, regime dependence, invalid representation, or an inconclusive result.

### Central hypothesis

A typed claim plus a regime-resolved audit will yield more reproducible and scientifically honest comparisons than an exponent-only universality score. In particular, the protocol predicts that some low-order inertial-range relations will be robust within homogeneous-isotropic card families, while high-order, anisotropic, wall, rotating, stratified, decaying, and measurement-limited cards will often require a conditional or regime-dependent status.

### Falsifiers

- The audit fails if it cannot distinguish an exact relation from a fitted empirical collapse.
- The hypothesis fails if the status is unstable under predeclared scale-window, estimator, or sampling changes.
- A universality claim fails if compatible baselines are missing or if a regime marker explains the apparent agreement.
- A transfer claim fails if it relies on pooled data without a documented symmetry and measurement bridge.

### Portfolio result

| Class | Direction | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | CURA-Turb claim-type and regime audit | Selected: covers all Survey gaps. |
| Competitive | Four-fifths-law residual benchmark | Retained as an exact-law control in eligible HIT cards. |
| High risk | Foundation-model universal turbulence embedding | Deferred: it risks hiding regimes and estimator choices behind a latent score. |
| Rejected | One-number universality index | Rejected: no assumption, scale, or transfer contract. |
