# Idea Agent: ORIGIN-OF-SPACETIME-INFERENCE-BENCHMARK

## Central idea

Build a transparent Bayesian inference benchmark that compares early-universe scenario
classes by their observable consequences rather than by an untestable story about a
location before space. The benchmark first tests the no-center consequence of homogeneous
expansion, then compares parameterized histories and initial perturbation states against
CMB, large-scale structure, gravitational-wave, and primordial-abundance likelihoods.

## Scenario representation

Every scenario supplies three objects:

1. a background expansion history, including any bounce, emergent phase, or inflationary
   epoch;
2. an initial-state prescription for scalar and tensor perturbations; and
3. a forward map from primordial quantities to observable maps and spectra.

The reference is a hot Big Bang plus a minimal inflationary or phenomenological
primordial-spectrum baseline. Alternatives must declare their extra parameters and
theoretical consistency conditions. A scenario with no distinct forward prediction is
classified as underdetermined, not as confirmed or rejected.

## Proposed task families

* **No-center geometry test:** simulate FLRW catalogs observed from many comoving
  locations; verify that recession and horizon observations do not select one center.
* **CMB likelihood task:** compare predicted temperature, polarization, lensing, and
  low-multipole distributions after foreground and instrument models.
* **Primordial-spectrum task:** compare tilt, running, oscillations, cutoffs,
  non-Gaussianity, and tensor signatures against predeclared windows.
* **Expansion-history task:** jointly evaluate CMB-derived constraints, baryon acoustic
  oscillations, supernova distances, and primordial nucleosynthesis consistency.
* **Topology and curvature task:** test spatial-curvature and topology signatures
  independently from an assumed explosion center.
* **Robustness task:** rerun inference under prior, foreground, likelihood, and data-split
  perturbations; disclose prediction failures and parameter degeneracies.

## Falsifiable hypotheses

* H0: the baseline hot Big Bang plus minimal primordial model explains the selected
  observations as well as more complex scenario classes after complexity and prior
  sensitivity are accounted for.
* H1: a parameterized early-universe scenario produces a posterior-predictive improvement
  on predeclared observables that persists across data and systematics choices.
* H2: apparent support for an early-universe feature decreases when look-elsewhere,
  foreground, and prior-volume effects are controlled.
* H3: topology, curvature, and finite horizon observations do not identify a spatial
  Big Bang center in a homogeneous model.
* H4: a past-incomplete inflationary spacetime requires a boundary description but does
  not select a unique physical origin mechanism.
* H5: information criteria and Bayes factors can rank predictive scenario classes, but
  cannot answer non-observable questions about "before time."

## Contribution and boundary

The benchmark's output is a scenario-by-observable profile: fit, posterior prediction,
complexity, parameter degeneracy, prior sensitivity, and resource cost. It treats a
model's past completion as a conditional theoretical claim. It does not manufacture an
origin event, a center, or a proof of creation from nothing. No data analysis is carried
out in this design-only work.
