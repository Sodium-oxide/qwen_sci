# Survey Agent: Where Did the Big Bang Start?

## 1. Scientific reframing

"Where did the Big Bang start?" presupposes that the event occurred at a location in a
pre-existing space. Standard homogeneous and isotropic cosmology does not make that
assumption. In an FLRW description, the scale factor changes the distances between
comoving locations throughout the spatial slice. The hot, dense early state is therefore
not an explosion from a central point into an external room. The relevant scientific
questions are:

> What observations establish the hot early universe and cosmic expansion; how far can
> the classical spacetime description be extrapolated; and which early-universe scenario
> classes make distinguishable predictions for observable relics?

This reframing separates spatial location, temporal extrapolation, causal boundary, and
physical mechanism. It does not assume that space or time existed before the regime
described by the standard hot Big Bang model.

## 2. Evidence map

### 2.1 What is strongly supported

The Planck 2018 cosmological-parameter analysis constrains a six-parameter flat Lambda
CDM model using the cosmic microwave background (CMB), with inferred age near 13.8 Gyr
[1]. Together with expansion, primordial element abundances, and CMB observations, this
supports a hot, dense early universe. It does not directly observe a time equal to zero
or identify a spatial starting point.

In a homogeneous cosmology, every comoving observer sees distant objects recede on
average because the scale factor grows. A finite observational horizon is not a center:
it is the limit set by light propagation and the universe's age. Any answer that assigns
the Big Bang to a presently identifiable galaxy, coordinate origin, or direction is
inconsistent with this model class unless a departure from homogeneity is explicitly
introduced and independently tested.

### 2.2 Inflation is a mechanism for selected early problems

Guth's inflationary proposal addresses the horizon and flatness problems of the standard
hot Big Bang scenario by a period of accelerated expansion [2]. Inflation provides a
framework for generating primordial perturbations and for relating early conditions to
CMB observables. It is not itself a demonstrated account of the ultimate beginning.

### 2.3 Classical extrapolation has a boundary

The Hawking-Penrose singularity theorems identify conditions under which classical
general-relativistic spacetimes are geodesically incomplete [3]. Borde, Guth, and
Vilenkin give a kinematical result that cosmological models that are inflating or
sufficiently expanding are incomplete in past-directed null and timelike directions
[4]. Their abstract states that inflation requires physics other than inflation to
describe the past boundary. This is a boundary statement, not a proof of creation from
nothing, a spatial point, or a unique pre-Big-Bang state.

### 2.4 Candidate completions remain model-dependent

Candidate mechanisms include inflationary initial-state proposals, quantum cosmology,
bouncing models, and emergent scenarios. Bouncing cosmology reviews describe both
potential mechanisms and unresolved challenges [5]. The scientific standard is not a
verbal origin story but a set of predictions that differ from a reference hot Big Bang
plus inflation model after nuisance parameters, priors, and systematics are controlled.

## 3. Definitions

* **Hot Big Bang:** the empirically supported early hot and dense expansion history,
  not necessarily a complete theory of spacetime origin.
* **Spatial center:** a distinguished physical location within a spatial slice. FLRW
  models have no such preferred center.
* **Coordinate origin:** a labeling convention; it is not automatically a physical
  origin.
* **Initial singularity:** a failure or boundary of a classical extrapolation, not a
  measured material point.
* **Past incompleteness:** the finite extension of certain past-directed geodesics under
  stated assumptions; it does not select a unique completion.
* **Early-universe scenario:** a parameterized model that supplies a background history
  and perturbation initial conditions.
* **Discriminating observable:** a measurement whose predicted distribution differs
  across scenario classes after instrumental, foreground, and late-time uncertainties
  are included.

## 4. Survey gaps

| Gap | Consequence | Control in proposed study |
|---|---|---|
| Question assumes external space | A false spatial center is inferred | State the spacetime and coordinate assumptions before analysis |
| Classical extrapolation is used beyond validity | Singularity is mistaken for a physical event | Separate data-supported epochs from model-dependent extrapolation |
| Scenario predictions overlap | Model choice becomes prior driven | Use posterior predictive checks and prior-sensitivity analyses |
| CMB anomalies are overinterpreted | A feature is mistaken for origin evidence | Predeclare multipole windows and foreground/systematics tests |
| Parameter tuning leaks into testing | Evidence is inflated | Freeze the pipeline before held-out frequency or data-split evaluation |
| Topology and curvature are conflated | Spatial shape is mistaken for a center | Analyze topology, curvature, and homogeneity as separate hypotheses |

## 5. Research questions

* RQ1: Under which spacetime assumptions is the phrase "where the Big Bang happened"
  a category error rather than an observable question?
* RQ2: How do expansion, CMB, and primordial-abundance data constrain the hot early
  universe without observing a temporal origin?
* RQ3: Can a data analysis distinguish a reference inflationary model from a bounce,
  emergent, or quantum-initial-state alternative using CMB and large-scale-structure
  observables?
* RQ4: Which observables - scalar spectrum features, spectral tilt and running,
  non-Gaussianity, spatial curvature, topology, or primordial tensor modes - have useful
  discriminating power after systematics and look-elsewhere controls?
* RQ5: Which conclusions remain conditional because observational access to a proposed
  past boundary is absent?

## 6. Evidence boundaries

The survey supports a direct answer: within the standard homogeneous model, the Big Bang
did not occur at one place in space. It describes an early expansion of space itself.
The age of the observable universe is inferred from a model constrained by data, not a
direct clock reading of an initial event. The mechanism producing the earliest physical
state remains unresolved. A successful scenario comparison can constrain model classes;
it cannot experimentally demonstrate "what happened before time" unless that phrase is
translated into a testable spacetime extension and observable prediction.
