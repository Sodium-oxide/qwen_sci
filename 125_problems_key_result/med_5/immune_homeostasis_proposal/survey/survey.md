# Survey Agent Output: How Is Immune Homeostasis Maintained and Regulated?

**Research question.** How is human immune homeostasis maintained across time and tissues, and what research platform can distinguish adaptive compensation from persistent dysregulation without treating the immune system as a single quantity to boost or suppress?

**Scope.** This survey concerns human immune variation, regulatory and barrier systems, host--microbiota crosstalk, immune memory, longitudinal multi-omics, and human-relevant microphysiological models. It is a research synthesis and proposal boundary. It does not provide a treatment protocol, recruit participants, manipulate pathogens, or claim that a model can diagnose a patient.

## Executive finding

Immune homeostasis is best understood as an **individualized, dynamic, compartmental feedback process**, not a fixed population-average cell count or cytokine threshold. It is maintained by the co-regulation of self-tolerance and regulatory programs, barrier and microbiota interactions, tissue positioning, hematopoietic renewal, prior exposure and immune memory, metabolism, and context-dependent effector responses. A healthy state is therefore not immobility: it is the ability to mount a proportionate response and return toward an individual's context-appropriate operating range without persistent tissue-damaging activation or loss of host defense.

The literature supports three strong propositions. First, baseline human immune states vary substantially between people, and parts of an individual's molecular profile can be comparatively stable over time [S1--S3]. Second, regulatory T-cell pathways and mucosal host--microbiota interactions are central mechanisms for balancing protection and tolerance [S4--S7]. Third, durable exposure-associated cellular and epigenetic states complicate any attempt to define a universal normal reference [S8]. The literature does **not** establish that one blood snapshot, one biomarker, an animal model, or a simplified in-vitro system can represent whole-body human immune homeostasis.

## Evidence map

| Evidence line | What it supports | Boundary on use |
|---|---|---|
| Human immune variation and vaccination studies | Healthy people differ meaningfully at baseline; baseline variables can relate to later response [S1,S2]. | A population mean cannot be treated as an individual's setpoint or clinical diagnosis. |
| Longitudinal wellness multi-omics | Interindividual diversity can coexist with within-individual longitudinal stability [S3]. | Stability in selected blood measurements is not whole-body immune stability. |
| Treg and tolerance literature | Regulatory circuits, including TGF-beta, IL-10, and CTLA-4-associated mechanisms, constrain inappropriate activation [S7]. | Preclinical transfer or depletion findings are not therapy instructions. |
| Microbiota and barrier literature | Microbiota, epithelium, and immune cells mutually shape development, containment, and inflammatory risk [S4--S6]. | Association with a microbial feature alone does not establish causality or a universal target. |
| Immune-memory studies | Infection history can leave persistent, cell-state-specific molecular footprints [S8]. | A historical signature is not necessarily maladaptive dysregulation. |
| Immunocompetent microphysiological systems | Human-relevant chips can model selected tissue--immune interactions and expose model limitations [S9--S11]. | Such systems do not reproduce a complete human immune system or prove clinical efficacy. |

## Accepted research gaps

1. **GAP-01 -- Static-reference problem.** Most measurements are interpreted against population reference intervals or cross-sectional cohorts, while homeostasis is a trajectory with person-specific variation and recovery dynamics.
2. **GAP-02 -- Cross-compartment integration problem.** Blood profiles, mucosal/barrier signals, microbiota, tissue context, and prior exposure are often measured separately and are difficult to align into one mechanistically interpretable state.
3. **GAP-03 -- Adaptation-versus-dysregulation problem.** A transient response, a durable memory state, and persistent pathological activation can all appear abnormal in a single snapshot.
4. **GAP-04 -- Human-causal-platform problem.** Animal and reductionist systems reveal mechanisms but cannot by themselves validate the feedback architecture of an individualized human immune system.
5. **GAP-05 -- Rebalancing-endpoint problem.** Broad immune enhancement or suppression lacks a principled, individual-specific endpoint for whether a response has been restored to an appropriate operating range.

## Evidence constraints for downstream agents

- Do not describe homeostasis as a universal fixed setpoint, a single biomarker, or an achieved clinical outcome.
- Do describe it as resilient, context-sensitive regulation across tolerance, effector, barrier, metabolic, and memory layers.
- Separate human observational evidence, animal-derived mechanisms, and microphysiological-platform evidence.
- Do not propose participant recruitment, treatment allocation, pathogen work, gene editing, or autonomous medical decision-making.
- Retain uncertainty, data coverage, tissue limitation, consent, privacy, and human expert review as explicit outputs.
