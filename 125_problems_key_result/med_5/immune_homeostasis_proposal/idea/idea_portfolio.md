# Idea Agent Output: Portfolio for Human Immune Homeostasis Research

## Problem reframing

The research problem is not to identify one normal immune number, nor to decide whether the immune system should generally be boosted or suppressed. The defensible problem is to estimate whether the coordinated immune state of one person is remaining within, departing from, or recovering toward a context-appropriate operating range -- while exposing the uncertainty created by incomplete tissue access, changing exposures, and measurement coverage.

## Candidate directions

| Direction | Core mechanism | Gap IDs | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1: Cross-sectional immune-health reference atlas | Establish population ranges across cell and molecular measurements. | GAP-01, GAP-02 | Competitive reference resource, but unable to distinguish adaptation from recovery. |
| **D2: Personal longitudinal homeostasis atlas with bounded human-platform counterfactual testing** | Learn a person's multi-layer operating range, detect resilience trajectories, and test selected feedback hypotheses in a nonclinical human microphysiological testbed. | **GAP-01--GAP-05** | **Selected primary direction.** |

| D4: Broad immune modulation to normalize the score | Treat score movement as an intervention endpoint. | GAP-05 | Rejected: no individual causal endpoint and unacceptable treatment overclaiming. |

## Simulated multi-route idea evolution

The first route treats homeostasis as a reference atlas. It is retained as a comparator because population ranges remain useful. The second route adds time, separating baseline variation from a response and recovery path. The third route adds a layered state representation so that tolerance, effector activation, barrier/microbial context, and immune memory cannot silently cancel one another in one score. The fourth route adds a bounded human microphysiological testbed for testing whether a proposed feedback relationship is directionally consistent outside an observational model. The combined direction is D2.

| Checkpoint | Candidate edit | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| I0 | Define one normal immune reference value. | Rejected: contradicted by human immune variation. |
| I1 | Build an individual longitudinal multi-omics baseline. | Retained: enables trajectory and recovery analysis. |
| I2 | Add compartment labels, tolerance/effector axes, microbial and memory context. | Retained: makes deviations more interpretable. |
| I3 | Add a human-relevant microphysiological comparator and abstention policy. | Selected: tests only bounded mechanisms and prevents diagnostic/therapeutic overclaiming. |

## Structured scientific debate

**Novelty critic:** D2 may merely combine existing multi-omics with organ-on-chip methods.  
**Response:** The contribution is the evaluation contract connecting individual trajectory, homeostatic axes, causal-test scope, and abstention. A large dataset or a chip without those links cannot tell whether a state is adaptive, dysregulated, or unobservable.

**Feasibility critic:** Whole-body tissue access is impossible in routine human studies.  
**Response:** D2 does not pretend otherwise. It treats unmeasured compartments as a documented limitation, produces a coverage-aware estimate, and restricts ex-vivo work to a predefined immune--barrier feedback question.

**Safety critic:** A personalized immune score could be misused as a diagnosis or as a directive to alter therapy.  
**Response:** D2 reports research-state labels only, requires ethics and clinical governance, disallows automated medical action, and makes the abstain state mandatory.

## Selected primary idea

**Title:** *A Personal Longitudinal Immune-Homeostasis Atlas with Bounded Human Microphysiological Counterfactual Testing*

**Central hypothesis.** A longitudinal, multi-layer model that represents personal baseline variation, tolerance--effector balance, barrier/microbiota context, and immune-memory history will distinguish resilient adaptation from persistent dysregulation candidates more transparently than a cross-sectional population score; bounded human microphysiological experiments can test selected predicted feedback relations but cannot establish clinical efficacy.

**Falsification conditions.** The direction is not supported if an individual model does not improve trajectory calibration or attribution over population references; if compartment and coverage uncertainty overwhelms the signal; if candidate feedback relations fail in the predefined nonclinical platform; or if the resulting states cannot be separated from ordinary biological variation.

**Primary contribution.** Replace the question ``is this immune measurement normal?'' with the testable question ``is this coordinated system responding and recovering in a way that is plausible for this individual, context, and coverage level?''
