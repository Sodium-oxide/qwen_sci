# Unifying Isolated Spin-Down and Binary Recycling in Massive Neutron Star Formation via Relaxed Equation of State Axioms

**Keywords:** neutron stars, equation of state, binary recycling, mass-spin relation, causality, TOV limit

## Abstract

This research plan introduces a candidate mechanism for massive neutron star formation by formally relaxing the static equilibrium axioms of the dense matter equation of state to incorporate rotational and thermal support from binary mass accretion. While existing observational constraints—such as the radius and mass bounds derived from GW170817—demonstrate the necessity of a sufficiently stiff equation of state to support massive remnants, standard static Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) limits remain contested by the population of millisecond pulsars exceeding 2.0 solar masses. The proposed framework unifies isolated spin-down and binary recycling pathways by modeling mass transfer as a continuous thermodynamic and mechanical perturbation. This modification predicts a distinct mass-spin relation, formalized as M = R(P), which differentiates binary-recycled massive neutron stars from isolated magnetic-dipole spin-down pulsars. The derivation is grounded in the assumption that companion ablation affects only the surface and magnetosphere, leaving the core governed by the relaxed effective equilibrium axioms.

The validity of the proposed mechanism is subject to strict boundary conditions, specifically the preservation of the causality bound (c_s <= c) and consistency with the observed population of massive millisecond pulsars. The research design prespecifies outcome branches that distinguish between support for the relaxed axioms and the sufficiency of standard rotational support models. A null or contradictory outcome would indicate that the relaxed static equilibrium axioms inevitably violate causality or tidal deformability constraints, or that standard stiff equations of state fully account for the mass-spin relation without requiring axiom relaxation. Consequently, the study advances a testable formal bridge between microphysical nucleosynthesis networks and macroscopic remnant properties, awaiting proof obligation verification to determine if the relaxed axioms can consistently produce observed massive neutron star masses within the defined validity domain.

## Introduction

### Scientific Setting and Observational Tension

The existence of massive neutron stars, particularly millisecond pulsars exceeding 2.0 solar masses, places stringent constraints on the equation of state (EoS) of dense matter. Standard binary evolution models attribute these high masses to a combination of a stiff EoS and rotational support acquired through binary spin-up, allowing remnants to exceed the static Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) limit. However, a critical gap remains in the mechanistic explanation of how these objects maintain stability without violating causality bounds or tidal deformability constraints. While gravitational-wave observations and pulsar timing provide robust empirical anchors for mass and spin parameters, the theoretical bridge linking binary accretion history to the effective equilibrium conditions of the neutron star core is underdeveloped. Current frameworks often treat rotational support as a post-hoc correction rather than a fundamental modification of the equilibrium axioms themselves. [@cite_42e341ff6471ce89] [@cite_a50fdc75b0400b49]

### Proposed Contribution: Relaxed Equilibrium Axioms

This research plan proposes a candidate mechanism that formally relaxes the static equilibrium axioms of the dense matter EoS to incorporate rotational and thermal support from binary mass accretion as continuous thermodynamic perturbations. The central hypothesis posits that this relaxation permits mass accumulation beyond the static TOV limit while preserving the causality bound (c_s <= c). By modeling binary accretion as a modification to the effective equilibrium conditions, rather than merely an angular momentum transfer event, this approach aims to unify isolated spin-down and binary recycling pathways. The proposed contribution is a distinct mass-spin relation, M = R(P), which predicts that massive neutron stars (>2.0 M_sun) with millisecond periods follow a specific evolutionary track distinguishable from isolated magnetic-dipole spin-down pulsars.

The study employs a mathematical/theoretical design, treating the theoretical model as the experimental unit. ## Background, Survey, and Research Gap

### Massive Neutron Stars and the Limits of Static Equations of State

The existence of massive neutron stars provides a stringent boundary condition for any theory of dense matter and stellar evolution. Radio-timing measurements of binary millisecond pulsars, specifically systems like PSR J1614-2230, demonstrate gravitational masses approaching two solar masses. These observations require an equation of state (EoS) for dense matter that is sufficiently stiff at supranuclear densities to prevent gravitational collapse. However, standard static Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) models struggle to reconcile these high masses with the simultaneous requirement of causal sound speeds and constraints from gravitational-wave events. The challenge is not merely numerical but structural: static equilibrium models assume spherical symmetry and non-rotation, whereas the observed massive remnants are rapidly spinning millisecond pulsars. This discrepancy necessitates a theoretical framework that explicitly incorporates rotational and thermal support into the equilibrium conditions, moving beyond the limitations of static TOV solutions. [@cite_42e341ff6471ce89] [@cite_55957f4ce67bd972]

### Rotational Support and Binary Recycling Dynamics

Binary recycling offers a mechanism to explain the high spin frequencies of these massive remnants. Accretion from a companion star transfers angular momentum, spinning up the neutron star and potentially increasing its maximum stable mass through centrifugal support. Population synthesis models indicate that binary interactions fundamentally alter progenitor trajectories, modulating mass loss and rotation rates. However, current modeling frameworks often treat the EoS and the rotational state as decoupled parameters. While the stiffening effect of rotation is acknowledged, it is typically applied as a post-hoc correction to static mass limits rather than being integrated into the fundamental equilibrium axioms. This separation obscures the precise dynamical conditions under which a neutron star can sustain masses beyond the static TOV limit without violating causality or triggering instability. The literature confirms that binary mass transfer efficiency and magnetic braking are major sources of uncertainty, further complicating the link between accretion history and the final mass-spin state. [@cite_1e5d552669fc0c56] [@cite_c369df652181e8f7]

### The Mechanistic Gap in Relaxed Equilibrium

A critical mechanism_explanation_gap persists in the relevant evidence cluster concerning massive neutron star masses. The survey evidence confirms that the 'candidate_mechanism' slot remains uncovered. While we observe massive millisecond pulsars and we possess static EoS models, there is no established formal theory that relaxes the static equilibrium axioms to incorporate rotational and thermal support in a way that preserves causality. Existing alternatives rely on standard stiff EoS combined with rotational support, but these models do not formally modify the underlying equilibrium conditions of the dense matter itself. Consequently, the field lacks a predictive mass-spin relation that distinguishes the evolutionary channel of binary recycling from isolated spin-down. This gap prevents a causal interpretation of how massive remnants form and survive, leaving the connection between microphysical EoS properties and macroscopic binary evolution underconstrained.

### Missing Premises for Causal Consistency

The proposed contribution addresses this gap by introducing a formal relaxation of static equilibrium axioms. This theoretical move requires specific missing premises to be established. First, the mathematical form of the relaxation must be defined such that it permits mass accumulation beyond the static limit without violating the speed-of-light constraint for sound propagation. Second, the continuous thermodynamic and mechanical perturbation caused by binary accretion must be modeled as a modification to the effective equilibrium conditions, not just an external torque. Third, the interaction between companion ablation and the core mass-spin relation must be characterized to ensure that surface effects do not invalidate the core equilibrium assumptions. These premises are currently absent from the frozen evidence, representing the precise intellectual labor required to bridge the gap between observed massive pulsars and theoretical dense matter physics.

## Study Design and Methods

The unit of analysis is a static theoretical model in which the equilibrium axioms of the dense-matter equation of state are relaxed to include rotational and thermal support from binary accretion. The protocol specifies three independent axes: the binary accretion parameters, the causality bound, and the equilibrium conditions. The dependent outputs are gravitational mass and spin period, reported in solar masses and milliseconds. The derivation begins by applying the relaxation to the static equilibrium constraint, then derives a candidate mass-spin relation, evaluates the mass limit at millisecond periods, checks the causality bound, and finally contrasts the resulting track with isolated magnetic-dipole spin-down. The protocol inherits the status of each step from the formal plan: the first step is proposed and the remaining steps are unverified, so the design is a verification schedule, not an established result. The design tests the relaxed-axiom mechanism against a declared alternative in which standard stiff equations of state plus rotational support from binary spin-up already account for massive millisecond pulsars without any relaxation of the fundamental nuclear axioms. The comparison is therefore a model-competition protocol: the relaxed-axiom track must produce a mass-spin relation that the standard-support track cannot reproduce within the same causal bound. Ablations isolate each control axis. Removing the rotational and thermal support term collapses the model to the static limit, and tightening the causality bound tests whether the predicted masses survive the speed-of-sound constraint. Robustness is treated as a numerical-implementation problem. The MESA binary module supplies the computational precedent for differentially rotating stars and implicit mass-transfer schemes, but it explicitly excludes common-envelope and contact-binary phases, so those phases are declared out of scope rather than silently absorbed. The Aquila comparison project demonstrates that code-to-code variation in subgrid physics can dominate the result even at fixed initial conditions; the design consequently requires a cross-implementation check before any mass-spin relation is treated as model-independent. ### Stress-Energy to Ricci: The Missing Bridge

The central gap in the proposed mechanism is the bridge from a dense-matter stress-energy tensor to a geometric Ricci/null-convergence condition. Under the Einstein equations, R_{ab}k^a k^b = 8\pi G (T_{ab} - \tfrac{1}{2}T g_{ab})k^a k^b; the factor (T_{ab} - \tfrac{1}{2}T g_{ab})k^a k^b equals T_{ab}k^a k^b because k is null. Thus NEC implies null convergence only if the field equations hold with the stated matter content and no additional geometric terms (e.g., a cosmological constant or modified-gravity corrections) contribute. The proposal's causality bound c_s \le c constrains the equation of state but does not supply this bridge. Establishing that the relaxed equilibrium axioms preserve NEC, and that NEC together with the field equations yields the required Ricci contraction, is an unverified proof obligation, not an observed result.
